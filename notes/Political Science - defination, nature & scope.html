📜 Definition of Political Science

🔴 Etymology & Core Idea

🌐 Polis (Greek: city-state) + Scire (Latin: to know) = "Knowledge of the State"

👑 Aristotle called it the "Master Science" because every other science requires a stable political community to flourish. He famously declared man a "Zoon Politikon" — a political animal — meaning humans only fulfill their nature inside the polis.

💎 The discipline historically grew out of moral philosophy (Plato, Aristotle) and later separated as an independent science in the late 19th century (founding of the American Political Science Association, 1903).

📜 Traditional Definitions (State-Centric)

Traditional thinkers viewed Political Science as essentially the study of the State — its origin, evolution, structure, and ends. The state was the alpha and omega of all political enquiry.

🧠 Thinker📖 Definition💡 Key Insight
Aristotle (384-322 BCE)"The study of the State (Polis) aimed at the highest human good — happiness (Eudaimonia)."State is a natural institution; man is a political animal.
Paul Janet 🇫🇷"That part of social science which treats of the foundations of the State and the principles of government."Foundations & Principles.
John W. Burgess 🇺🇸"The study of the State in the past, present, and future — its organization, powers, and purposes."Historical + Structural lens.
James W. Garner ⭐"Political Science begins and ends with the State."The PUREST State-centric definition (most quoted in exams).
Stephen Leacock 🇨🇦"The study of government and the State — how men rule and are ruled."Practical & dynamic flavour.
Bluntschli 🇩🇪"The science concerned with the State, which endeavours to understand it in its essential nature, forms, and development."Organic / evolutionary view of the State.
R.G. Gettell"Political Science is the study of the State in its past, present and future, of political organisation, of political functions and of political theories."Holistic state focus.
Sir Frederick PollockDivided it into theoretical (state & sovereignty) and practical (govt & legislation) politics.Theory + Practice split.

💡 Modern Definitions (Power & Decision-Centric)

Post-1945, scholars began arguing the State was too narrow a unit. They redefined Political Science around power, authority, decision-making and the allocation of values — making it more empirical and behavioural.

🎁

📦

🎯 INTEGRATED DEFINITION (Copy this in Exam!)

Political Science is the systematic study of the State, Government, Power, Authority, and Decision-making processes that allocate scarce values in society — using scientific methods combined with philosophical insight.

⚖️ Nature of Political Science

The biggest academic battle in this field has been: "Is Political Science truly a SCIENCE, or merely an ART?" Let's dissect both sides scientifically. 🔬

🔬 FOR — It IS a Science

  • Empirical Observation — Elections, parliaments, wars are observable. (Bryce)
  • Formulation of Laws — Michels' Iron Law of Oligarchy, Duverger's Law (electoral), Parkinson's Law, Acton's Law.
  • Comparative Method — Compare democracies vs dictatorships scientifically. (Almond & Powell)
  • Prediction — Opinion polls & psephology forecast election results.
  • Quantitative Tools — Statistics, game theory, median voter theorem (Anthony Downs).
  • Systematic Body of Knowledge — Has cumulative theories and verifiable hypotheses.

🎨 AGAINST — It's NOT a Science

  • Human Behaviour is Unpredictable — Revolutions are spontaneous; minds change.
  • No Laboratory — Cannot isolate variables like physics or chemistry.
  • Value-Laden — Researchers' ideology colors conclusions.
  • No Universal Laws — "Sovereignty" itself is fluid in a globalised world.
  • Comte, Maitland, Buckle, Mosca argued politics lacks exactness, measurement and certainty.
  • Ethical & Normative — Concerns "ought" not just "is".
✅ Modern Consensus (Resolution)

David Easton: Political Science is a "Science of Politics" — systematic, empirical, methodical — but a human science (Geisteswissenschaft), not a natural one.

Robert Dahl: "It is a science in the sense that it is a systematic, empirical, and verifiable study."

👉 So the final answer: Political Science is a SOCIAL SCIENCE — scientific in method, artistic in subject matter.

⚙️ The Behavioural Revolution (1950s)

🌪️ Born from frustration with the dry legal-formal approach (which only studied constitutions). After WWII, American scholars demanded the study of actual human political behaviour — how voters vote, how legislators legislate, how leaders lead.

📚 Founding text: David Easton's "The Political System" (1953). Key contributors: Charles Merriam (the "Chicago School" pioneer), Lasswell, Almond, Dahl, Kaplan.

🎯 Easton's 8 Characteristics of Behavioralism (the "Intellectual Foundation Stones")

#🔑 Pillar📖 Meaning
1RegularitiesDiscover uniformities/patterns in political behaviour that allow generalisation.
2VerificationValidity of theories must be testable by empirical evidence.
3TechniquesUse sophisticated tools — surveys, sampling, interviewing, content analysis.
4QuantificationMeasure political phenomena (power index, voting %, GINI of representation).
5ValuesMaintain ethical neutrality — separate facts from values (value-free research).
6SystematizationConnect theory and research systematically — no isolated facts.
7Pure ScienceKnowledge for its own sake first; application later.
8IntegrationMerge with sociology, psychology, economics, anthropology.

🔄 Post-Behavioralism (1969 onwards)

Behavioralism became over-quantified, narrow, and detached from real-world crises — Vietnam War, racial unrest, poverty. David Easton himself launched the corrective movement with his 1969 APSA Presidential Address.

🔥 Slogan: "Relevance over Rigour" — politics must speak to urgent human issues.

📜 Easton's "Credo of Relevance" — 7 Tenets:

  1. Substance over technique — meaningful research over precise methods.
  2. Social change over preservation of status quo.
  3. Contact with brute realities of politics.
  4. Values are essential — cannot be evaded.
  5. Protection of humane values is duty of intellectuals.
  6. Action-oriented research.
  7. Politicization of professions — scholars must engage with society.

⚖️ Final Verdict — Balanced Table

AspectTypeWhy
Methodology🔬 ScientificSystematic, empirical, verifiable
Subject Matter🎨 ArtisticDeals with human behaviour, values, judgement
Goal🤝 BothDescribe (science) + Prescribe (art)
Status🌐 Social ScienceDistinct from natural sciences, but scientific nonetheless
🎯 Exam Tip

If asked "Is Political Science a science?" — Answer: It is a social science — it uses scientific methods to study political behaviour but cannot claim the exactness of physics. Accept both science and art aspects; that's the safest, fullest answer.

🗺️ Scope of Political Science

The scope answers — "What all does Political Science study?" It has expanded massively from the State-only focus of the 19th century to the multi-dimensional discipline of today.

🌟 UNESCO Classification (1948, Paris) — Landmark Moment

The UNESCO conference of 1948 in Paris formally recognised Political Science as an autonomous discipline and divided its scope into 4 broad areas:

  1. Political Theory — concepts, ideas, history of political thought.
  2. Political Institutions — constitution, government, comparative institutions.
  3. Parties, Groups & Public Opinion — political dynamics.
  4. International Relations — IR, IL, international organisations.

📜 1. Political Theory — The Backbone

BranchFocusKey ConceptsThinkers
NormativeWhat ought to be (values/data)Justice, Equality, Liberty, Rights, DemocracyPlato, Aristotle, Rousseau, Marx, Rawls
EmpiricalWhat is (facts/data)Power, Authority, Legitimacy, DecisionsLasswell, Easton, Dahl, Weber
AnalyticalClarity of concepts, what words mean (logic/concepts)"Political", "Freedom", "Two Concepts of Liberty"Isaiah Berlin, Hannah Arendt
CriticalEmancipatory critique, how power oppresses (freedom/change)Power-knowledge, hegemony, ideologyFrankfurt School, Foucault, Gramsci

🔑 POWER — A's ability to make B do what B would not otherwise do. (Dahl)

🔑 AUTHORITY — Legitimate power, accepted by subjects. (Weber)

🔑 LEGITIMACY — Belief that rule is rightful. Weber's 3 types:

  • 👴 Traditional — custom, heredity (monarchy)
  • ⭐ Charismatic — personality magnetism (Gandhi, Napoleon)
  • 📜 Legal-Rational — rules, constitution (modern democracies)

🔑 SOVEREIGNTY — Supreme power of the State. Internal (over people) + External (independence). Thinkers: Jean Bodin (originator, 1576), John Austin (Monistic), Harold Laski (Pluralist critique).

🏛️ 2. Study of State & Government

🧱 Four Elements of State (P-GST mnemonic):

  1. Population — the people
  2. Territory — defined geographical area
  3. Government — agency of authority
  4. Sovereignty — supreme power, internal + external

⚖️ 5. Public Administration

🏢 Definition: The implementation of government policies — "Public Administration is detailed and systematic application of law." (Woodrow Wilson, 1887, "The Study of Administration").

🧠 Wilson's Politics-Administration Dichotomy: Politics decides; administration implements. (Now challenged — they overlap.)

📜 Max Weber's Ideal Bureaucracy: Hierarchy, Division of labour, Rules, Impersonality, Merit-based recruitment, Career service, Documentation.

🚀 New Public Management (NPM) — 1980s: Market-oriented reforms; "Steering not rowing" (Osborne & Gaebler, Reinventing Government).

🌱 Development Administration — Riggs, Weidner: Administration as engine of nation-building in the Third World.

🤝 New Public Service (NPS) — Denhardt: Citizens are not customers but partners; democratic ethos.

📊 6. Comparative Politics

📈 Old vs New: Traditional "Comparative Government" only studied European constitutions; modern Comparative Politics studies all systems — Asian, African, Latin American, micro-states, traditional societies.

🔬 Methods: Mill's Methods (Most Similar / Most Different Systems Design), Case studies, Statistical analysis, Process tracing.

📚 Landmark works: Almond & Verba — The Civic Culture (1963); Almond & Powell — Comparative Politics: A Developmental Approach; Lijphart — Patterns of Democracy.

🧠 7. Political Methodology

📊 Quantitative

Surveys 📋, experiments 🧪, statistical inference 📈 (regression, factor analysis), simulations, large-N studies.

📝 Qualitative

Process tracing 🔍, interviews 🎙️, discourse analysis 💬, ethnography, case studies, archival research.

🎲 Game Theory: Rational choice models — Prisoner's Dilemma applied to arms races, Chicken Game, Nash equilibrium.

💻 Digital Methods: Social media analysis (X/Twitter sentiment), big data, NLP, text mining, network analysis of political alliances.

🔄 David Easton's Systems Theory (Most Asked!)

Political system receives INPUTS (demands + supports from society) → undergoes CONVERSION inside the "black box" of government → produces OUTPUTS (policies, laws) → which generate FEEDBACK that becomes new inputs. The system constantly adapts to maintain equilibrium.
