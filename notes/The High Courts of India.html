01
The constitutional architecture

🏛️ What exactly is a High Court?

A High Court is a constitutional court at the head of the ordinary judicial hierarchy of a State (or group of States / Union Territories). It sits directly beneath the Supreme Court in India's integrated judiciary, yet it is not administratively controlled by the Supreme Court — it controls the courts below it. And it is older: the first three High Courts opened in 1862, the Supreme Court in 1950 — High Courts predate it by 1950 − 1862 = 88 years.

🇮🇳 Supreme CourtApex court · Arts. 124–147
🏛️ 25 High CourtsConstitutional courts · Arts. 214–231 · control below via Art. 235
⚖️ District & subordinate courtsWhere most litigation begins · Arts. 233–237
Art. 214

🏠 A High Court for each State

There shall be a High Court for each State. This guarantees a constitutional High Court for every State — but not an exclusive one: a common High Court is allowed.

Art. 231

🤝 One court, many States

Parliament may by law establish a common High Court for two or more States, or for States and a Union Territory (e.g. Punjab & Haryana + Chandigarh; Bombay for Maharashtra, Goa and a UT).

Art. 230

🧭 Reach into Union Territories

Parliament may extend a High Court's jurisdiction to a UT, or exclude it from a UT. (Kerala ➜ Lakshadweep; Madras ➜ Puducherry.)

Art. 241

🏙️ High Courts for UTs

Parliament may constitute a High Court for a UT or declare an existing court to be one. Delhi has its own High Court (1966); J&K and Ladakh share one.

From Crown charters to constitutional courts

🕰️ Origin & evolution

Two colonial judicial streams — the Crown's Supreme Courts in the Presidency towns and the Company's Sadar Adalats — were merged by the Indian High Courts Act, 1861, which authorised High Courts created by Letters Patent. That architecture still stands today.

People, offices and temporary arrangements

👩‍⚖️ Composition, qualifications & types of judges

🏗️ Art. 216 — flexible strengthA High Court consists of a Chief Justice and such other judges as the President deems necessary from time to time. No uniform number is fixed by the Constitution. Sanctioned strength ≠ working strength (vacancies are the gap). Contrast the Supreme Court, whose maximum is fixed by Parliament (Art. 124(1)).

🎓 Qualifications — Art. 217(2)

🇮🇳

Citizen of India

Residence in the State is not a constitutional qualification.

⚖️

10 years of judicial office

Held a judicial office in the territory of India for at least ten years — OR…

📚

10 years as HC advocate

Advocate of a High Court (or two or more in succession) for at least ten years. The Explanation counts certain related periods.

🧠 Mnemonic · C-10-10

Citizen + 10 years on the Bench or 10 years at the Bar. No minimum age. No “distinguished jurist” route — that exists only for the Supreme Court (Art. 124(3)(c)).

Constitutional text + judicial interpretation

🤝 Appointment, collegium & transfer

Art. 217(1): the President appoints, by warrant under hand and seal, after consulting the Chief Justice of India, the Governor and — for a judge other than the Chief Justice — the Chief Justice of the High Court. In practice, “consultation” was interpreted into a collegium of judges who form the recommendation.

👑 Chief Justices

The President appoints a High Court Chief Justice after the same consultative process (CJI + 2 senior-most). Appointing Chief Justices from outside the State is a long-standing convention, not an eligibility condition in Art. 217. The senior-most judge has no automatic right to become permanent Chief Justice.

🔄 Art. 222 — Transfer of judges

⚙️ The rule

The President may transfer a judge (including a Chief Justice) from one High Court to another after consulting the CJI. Art. 222(2): a compensatory allowance is payable.

⚖️ The safeguards

Transfer must serve public interest (better administration of justice) and not be punitive. The judge's consent is not constitutionally required (Sankalchand Sheth, 1977). Judicial review is narrow and process-focused.

06
Conditions that protect the office

💰 Oath, tenure, pay & practice restrictions

Art. 219

🤚 Oath or affirmation

Made and subscribed before the Governor or a person appointed by the Governor, in the Third Schedule form: true faith and allegiance to the Constitution, upholding India's sovereignty and integrity, duties performed without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.

Art. 217(1), (3)

🎂 Tenure

Till 62. Office is vacated on: (a) resignation in writing to the President; (b) removal by the Art. 124(4) route; (c) appointment as a Supreme Court judge or transfer to another High Court (Art. 217(1)(c)). Any question about a judge's age is decided by the President after consulting the CJI, and that decision is final (Art. 217(3), inserted in 1963).

💰 Who fixes the pay — and which fund actually pays?

Article 221 separates salary from allowances, leave and pension rights. Parliament fixes salaries by law. Allowances and rights in respect of leave and pension are determined by or under parliamentary law, and the proviso protects those allowances and rights from being varied to the judge's disadvantage after appointment. Keep the source of the law (Art. 221) separate from the source of payment (Arts. 202 and 112).

🚨 Financial Emergency — Art. 360

During a Financial Emergency, the President may direct reductions in the salaries and allowances of persons serving the Union, including Supreme Court and High Court judges. This is an exceptional constitutional power, not an ordinary executive power to cut judicial pay.

🚪 Article 220 — what may a former permanent judge practise?

✅

Permitted forums

A person who has held office as a permanent High Court judge may plead or act in the Supreme Court and in other High Courts, subject to professional rules.

⛔

Closed forums

The person may not plead or act in their own former High Court, in subordinate courts, or before other authorities in India. Describing this as “no practice anywhere” is wrong.

🎯 HC ≠ SC after retirementArt. 220: former permanent HC judge → Supreme Court + other High Courts allowed. Art. 124(7): former SC judge → may not plead or act before any court or authority in India. Neither provision is a blanket rule on post-retirement public offices.

Accountability without executive dismissal

⚖️ Removal, inquiry & accountability

No Governor, Cabinet, Chief Justice or CJI can dismiss a High Court judge. Art. 217(1)(b), read with Art. 218, applies the removal framework of Art. 124(4) and (5). The only grounds are proved misbehaviour and incapacity.

🧠 Mnemonic · 100–50 → 3 → Double majority → President

100 LS / 50 RS start it; 3 investigate; both Houses need the two-part special majority; the President removes. A committee report is not itself a removal.

🕵️ In-house inquiry is not parliamentary removal

The judiciary's in-house procedure examines allegations of misconduct internally. Its outcomes can include advice to resign, a change in judicial work allocation, or forwarding material for constitutional action. It does not empower the CJI to remove a constitutional judge. Criminal investigation, contempt and parliamentary removal are separate processes. Judicial independence is not a general immunity from criminal law, but the safeguards on starting proceedings against superior-court judges must be read with K. Veeraswami v. Union of India (1991).

📚 V. Ramaswami · 1993

The Lok Sabha motion did not gain the required majority. He was a Supreme Court judge when the motion was voted on.

📚 Soumitra Sen · 2011

The Rajya Sabha adopted the motion concerning the Calcutta High Court judge. He resigned before the Lok Sabha completed the process.

🧭 Live proceedings

Notices, committee reports, resignations and court challenges change quickly. A notice or news report is not proof that removal has happened. Check dated official records.

🗣️ Terminology matters“Impeachment of a judge” is common shorthand. The Constitution speaks of removal. Impeachment strictly belongs to the President (Art. 61). As far as the verified record goes, no High Court judge has been removed through the completed procedure. Resignation, lapse and refusal of a notice are not the same as removal.
08
An institutional guarantee to the litigant

🛡️ Independence — structural, financial & functional

Independence protects a court's ability to decide against powerful parties, including the government. It is not personal immunity from criticism or accountability. Independence and reasoned, transparent adjudication strengthen each other.

🏰

Structural safeguards

Arts. 217–218: protected tenure and difficult removal. Collegium jurisprudence limits executive primacy in appointments. Transfers under Art. 222 need consultation with the CJI.

💰

Financial safeguards

Arts. 221, 202, 112: parliamentary pay-setting, protected leave and pension rights, and charged (non-votable) expenditure. Financial Emergency is the express exception.

🎛️

Administrative autonomy

Art. 229: appointments to the court's establishment rest with the Chief Justice or an authorised judge or officer. Service rules on salary, allowances, leave and pension need the Governor's approval.

🌳

Protection below

Art. 235: High Court control over district and subordinate courts shields the judicial service from day-to-day executive control.

📜

Constitutional review

Arts. 226–227: judicial review cannot be wholly displaced by ordinary legislation creating alternative forums (L. Chandra Kumar).

⚖️

Authority of the court

Art. 215: court-of-record status and contempt power support the administration of justice. Fair criticism is not automatically contempt.

🗣️ Articles 121 and 211 — a useful asymmetry

Art. 121 bars discussion in Parliament on the conduct of a Supreme Court or High Court judge in discharging duties, except on a motion for an address praying for removal. Art. 211 imposes the same bar on State Legislatures and has no removal exception, because State Legislatures play no part in removing superior-court judges.

🔓 Accountability that coexists with independence

Reasoned judgments, open hearings, appeal and review, recusal where appropriate, judicial ethics, lawful asset-disclosure arrangements, statutory access to information, and the removal framework all contribute to accountability. An open court does not mean every case may be broadcast without restriction. Privacy, safety and court rules still apply.

09
The working toolkit of constitutional justice

🔍 Jurisdiction, judicial review & the five writs

High Courts combine constitutional remedies with ordinary civil and criminal adjudication. Their ordinary jurisdiction depends on the Constitution, Letters Patent where applicable, central and State statutes, and rules of court. Not every High Court has the same original civil jurisdiction.

🏛️ 9.1 Original jurisdiction — where proceedings begin

🕊️ Constitutional petitions

Petitions under Art. 226 begin in the High Court. They may challenge unlawful detention, administrative action, legislation, or failure to perform a public duty, within limits of maintainability and territorial jurisdiction.

🗳️ Election petitions

Disputes over elections to Parliament and State legislatures go to High Courts under the Representation of the People Act, 1951 (s.80A). Presidential and Vice-Presidential disputes go to the Supreme Court (Art. 71).

💼 Ordinary original civil side

Some courts — Bombay, Calcutta, Madras and Delhi among them — exercise ordinary original civil jurisdiction within statutory limits. It is wrong to say every high-value suit anywhere begins in a High Court.

⚓ Special statutory jurisdiction

Admiralty, commercial, arbitration and company-related matters depend on specific legislation. Tribunal reallocations mean “all company cases go to the HC” is no longer reliable.

📜 9.2 Article 226 — Fundamental Rights and “any other purpose”

A High Court may issue directions, orders or writs, including the five named writs, to any person or authority and, in suitable cases, to a government. It covers Part III rights and other legally enforceable rights. “Any other purpose” does not allow adjudication of purely moral complaints with no legal foundation.

These are memory cues, not legal tests. Art. 226 also allows “directions” and “orders”, so a High Court's remedial power is not confined to the old technical writs.

🧭 9.3 Territorial reach — Art. 226(2)

A High Court may act where the cause of action arises wholly or in part within its territory, even if the authority is seated elsewhere. The territorial facts must be material to the dispute. Residence, or an incidental communication received in the State, does not by itself create jurisdiction (Kusum Ingots, 2004).

🗺️ ExampleAn authority outside State A takes a decision whose legally relevant effect arises in State A. Depending on the material facts, State A's High Court may have jurisdiction. The respondent's headquarters alone does not settle it.

🚧 9.4 Alternative remedies, delay and public-law character

Art. 226 is an extraordinary and discretionary jurisdiction. Courts ordinarily expect an effective statutory remedy to be used first, but that is a rule of self-restraint, not an absolute bar. Recognised exceptions include a Fundamental Rights issue, a breach of natural justice, proceedings wholly without jurisdiction, and a challenge to the vires of legislation: Whirlpool (1998); Radha Krishan Industries (2021). Delay, suppression of facts, disputed questions of evidence and the public-law character of the duty also matter (Andi Mukta, 1989).

⏱️ 9.5 Ex parte interim orders — the exact Art. 226(3) rule

Suppose an interim order is made against a party without giving that party the petition and supporting documents and without a hearing. That party may apply to vacate it and furnish a copy to the beneficiary or their counsel. The court must dispose of the application within two weeks from receipt or from furnishing the copy, whichever is later (with a court-closure qualification). If it does not, the interim order stands vacated.

🚨 Not “every stay ends in two weeks”Art. 226(3) applies only to its stated ex parte order + vacation application + service situation. It is not a general two-week sunset for every writ stay.

🤲 9.6 PIL and public-law compensation

Public Interest Litigation relaxes traditional standing for genuine public injury, especially where affected people cannot approach the court themselves. Good faith and credible material remain essential. Service disputes and private rivalries cannot ordinarily be repackaged as PIL. High Courts may award public-law compensation for established rights violations such as unlawful detention or custodial abuse (Rudul Sah, 1983; Nilabati Behera, 1993; D.K. Basu, 1997). That is distinct from a full private-law damages trial.

🌳 9.7 Article 227 — superintendence, not a second appeal

Every High Court has superintendence over courts and tribunals throughout its territorial jurisdiction, except those under laws relating to the Armed Forces. It also covers calling for returns, making rules and prescribing forms for practice and proceedings. Such rules and fee tables must be consistent with law and need the Governor's previous approval. The power is meant to keep subordinate adjudication within lawful bounds and is exercised sparingly (Waryam Singh, 1954; Shalini Shyam Shetty, 2010).

🧩 9.8 Article 228 — constitutional questions in lower courts

If a case pending in a subordinate court involves a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the Constitution, and its determination is necessary for disposing of the case, the High Court shall withdraw it. It may decide the whole case itself, or decide the constitutional question and return the case to the lower court with its judgment.

🎯 “Shall”, but only once the conditions are metArt. 228 is not a general power to withdraw any interesting case. The constitutional question must be substantial and necessary for disposal.

📈 9.9 Appellate, revisional and review powers

💼 Civil appeals

A first appeal ordinarily allows examination of fact and law under the governing statute. A second appeal under CPC s.100 needs a substantial question of law — “only law” is too loose. Intra-court appeals depend on the governing law or Letters Patent.

🔒 Criminal appeals and death references

Appellate and revisional routes are statutory. A Sessions Court's death sentence needs High Court confirmation, which is separate from the accused's appeal. The BNSS, 2023 replaced the CrPC from 1 July 2024, subject to transitional provisions.

🛠️ Revision

Revision examines jurisdictional or other specified serious legal defects within statutory limits. It is narrower than a regular appeal and is not a licence to reassess all the evidence.

🔄 Review

Review is different from appeal. Availability depends on the jurisdiction exercised and the governing law. Final criminal orders are subject to strict statutory limits.

📚 9.10 Court of record, contempt and precedent

Art. 215 makes every High Court a court of record with power to punish for contempt of itself. Under the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, High Courts also deal with contempt of subordinate courts. Civil contempt concerns wilful disobedience or breach of an undertaking; criminal contempt concerns the statutorily defined scandalising of the court, prejudice to proceedings, or obstruction of justice. Fair reporting and fair criticism are protected, and truth can be a defence in public interest (s.13, as amended in 2006). Contempt is therefore not simply “saying something negative about a judge”.

A High Court's declared law binds subordinate courts in its territory. Another High Court's decision is ordinarily persuasive, not binding. Within a High Court, a larger bench prevails over a smaller one. Every High Court is bound by the Supreme Court under Art. 141.

Part VI · Chapter VI · Articles 233–237

🌳 The subordinate judiciary — independence at the roots

Most people meet justice first in a district court. Articles 233–237 protect this foundation by dividing roles among the Governor, the High Court and the State Public Service Commission.

Art. 233(1)

👩‍⚖️ District judges

Appointment, posting and promotion of district judges are made by the Governor in consultation with the High Court. The consultation must be real and effective.

Art. 233(2)

📚 Direct entry from the Bar

A person not already in Union or State service is eligible only if an advocate or pleader for at least seven years and recommended by the High Court. A serving judicial officer cannot use this quota (Dheeraj Mor, 2020).

Art. 234

🧑‍💼 Judicial service below district judge

The Governor appoints under rules made after consulting the State Public Service Commission and the High Court.

Art. 235

🛡️ Control

Control over district courts and subordinate courts is vested in the High Court, including posting, promotion and leave for posts below district judge. It also covers disciplinary control (State of W.B. v. Nripendra Nath Bagchi, 1966), subject to service-law safeguards.

🔄 Appointment versus controlThe Governor's formal role does not make the subordinate judiciary an executive department. Equally, High Court control does not erase the Governor's formal role where the Constitution or service law reserves the final order to that authority.

📖 Articles 233A, 236 and 237

Art. 233A (20th Amendment, 1966) validates specified past appointments and acts after Chandra Mohan v. State of U.P. (1966). It is not a permanent excuse for skipping consultation. Art. 236 defines “district judge” inclusively (including additional district judges, sessions judges, assistant sessions judges and other listed equivalents) and “judicial service” as a service made up exclusively of persons meant to fill the post of district judge and other civil judicial posts inferior to it. Art. 237 lets the Governor, by public notification, apply this Chapter and its rules to specified classes of magistrates, with modifications.

🇮🇳 All India Judicial Service — a framework, not an existing cadre

Art. 312 allows an All India Judicial Service through a Rajya Sabha resolution and parliamentary law. Under Art. 312(3) it cannot include any post inferior to district judge as defined in Art. 236. Proposals for it should not be presented as an operational replacement for State judicial services. Art. 50, the Directive Principle on separating the judiciary from the executive, explains why judicial-service control is not ordinary civil-service management.

🏛️ High Courts as constitutional laboratories

🏳️‍🌈 Naz Foundation · Delhi HC · 2009

The Delhi High Court read down IPC s.377 for consensual adult same-sex conduct. The Supreme Court reversed it in Koushal (2013), and the Constitution Bench in Navtej Singh Johar (2018) overruled Koushal. Always cite the later history.

⛓️ Habeas corpus in the Emergency

Several High Courts held that detention could still be examined. The Supreme Court majority in ADM Jabalpur (1976) took the opposite view, later expressly repudiated in Puttaswamy (2017).

⚔️ Supreme Court versus High Courts

“Higher” does not mean “the same powers, only more”. A High Court has territorial supervisory and service-control functions that the Supreme Court does not have as a general power. The Supreme Court has apex appellate and other unique jurisdictions.

Capacity, credibility and access

🛠️ Challenges, reforms & the verification desk

More judges help, but delay comes from a whole chain: vacancies, registry processes, service of notice, incomplete records, adjournments, government litigation, tribunal design, infrastructure, and weak enforcement. A strong answer addresses the entire chain.

⏳

Vacancies & workload

Track sanctioned strength, working strength and vacancies separately. Timely initiation of recommendations and predictable processing help. Raw cases-per-judge averages hide differences in complexity.

🗂️

Case management

Early issue-framing, realistic scheduling, disciplined adjournments, reliable service and effective registry scrutiny cut avoidable delay without sacrificing a fair hearing.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Diversity & transparency

Gender, social background, professional experience and regional representation affect institutional legitimacy. Published criteria and reasoned procedures can build trust while protecting legitimate confidentiality.

🗺️

Distance, language & cost

Benches, circuit sittings, legal aid, translation and hybrid hearings widen access. A bench also needs staff, infrastructure and a lawful allocation. An announcement is not an operational bench.

💻

eCourts & technology

E-filing, searchable orders, electronic records, video hearings and data dashboards help administration. Accessibility, cybersecurity, digital exclusion and translation accuracy must be handled. AI output needs human verification.

🤝

ADR & better governance

Mediation, Lok Adalats and settlement suit appropriate disputes. Rights-sensitive matters need safeguards. Better government decision-making can prevent litigation.

👴

Art. 224A capacity

Retired judges can help with arrears, but they should supplement, not replace, timely regular appointments. Apply the latest Supreme Court directions, not an outdated threshold.

🛡️

Accountability with independence

Ethical standards, reasoned orders, credible complaint processes and recusal practice must coexist with protection against retaliatory pressure.

📊 Read judicial statistics intelligently

Pendency is the stock of unresolved cases on a date. Institution counts new cases in a period and disposal counts resolved ones. Disposal above institution can reduce the stock, but transfers, restoration and reporting scope affect comparisons. Always record the court level, date, case type and source, and never mix one High Court's figures with all-court totals.
