ARTICLE 324

Superintendence, Direction & Control of Elections - Constitutional Foundation
Detailed Constitutional Framework

Article 324(1) - Vesting of Electoral Powers

Explicitly vests the superintendence, direction, and control of all elections to:

  • Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha)
  • State Legislatures (Vidhan Sabha & Vidhan Parishad)
  • Offices of President and Vice-President of India

This clause establishes the ECI as the supreme electoral authority with comprehensive jurisdiction over the entire electoral process in India

‎

Constitutional Authority & Scope
Explicit Vesting of Power
Article 324(1) explicitly vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in an Election Commission (ECI).
Threefold Authority
Superintendence = Overall supervision
Direction = Issuing guidelines & instructions
Control = Ensuring compliance & enforcement
Jurisdictional Scope
Applies to elections for:
• Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha)
• State Legislatures (Vidhan Sabha & Vidhan Parishad)
• President & Vice-President of India

Article 324(2) - Composition of Election Commission

States that the Election Commission shall consist of:

  • Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) - The head of the Commission
  • Other Election Commissioners - As the President may from time to time fix

This provision allows for flexibility in the Commission's composition based on electoral demands and workload.

Article 324(3) - Chairman of the Commission

Specifies that when other Election Commissioners are appointed, the Chief Election Commissioner acts as the Chairman of the Election Commission.

This establishes the CEC as primus inter pares (first among equals) in the multi-member Commission structure.

Article 324(4) - Regional Commissioners

Provides for the appointment of Regional Commissioners to assist the Election Commission, after consultation with the Election Commission.

These commissioners help in administering elections at regional levels and ensure decentralized electoral management.

Article 324(5) - Conditions of Service & Tenure

Governs the conditions of service and tenure of the Commissioners with crucial protections:

  • CEC Protection: Cannot be removed from office except in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court
  • Other ECs: Cannot be removed from office except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner
  • Service conditions cannot be varied to the incumbent's disadvantage after appointment

Article 324(6) - Administrative Support

Mandates that the President or the Governor of a State shall, when so requested by the Election Commission, make available to the Election Commission such staff as may be necessary for the discharge of its functions.

This ensures the ECI has the necessary administrative machinery to conduct elections effectively across the country.

Composition & Evolution
1950
ECI established as a single-member body with only Chief Election Commissioner
1974
Temporary appointment of second Election Commissioner (T. Swaminathan)
1989
Two more ECs added (S.S. Dhanoa & V.S. Seigell) alongside voting age reduction from 21 to 18
1990
Posts of additional ECs abolished by President's order
1993
Restored as multi-member body (3 ECs) via ordinance; continues today
2023
Supreme Court judgment in Anoop Baranwal case recommends appointment committee
Key Insight: The Election Commission operates as an independent constitutional authority - not a department under any ministry, ensuring its autonomy from executive influence. Article 324 provides the constitutional bedrock for India's electoral democracy.
Appointment, Tenure & Removal
Appointment Process
• By President of India (executive power)
• No parliamentary approval required
• No fixed qualification criteria in Constitution
• 2023 Reform: Supreme Court recommended 3-member committee (PM, LoP, CJI) until Parliament enacts law
Tenure & Service Conditions
• 6 years or until age 65 (whichever earlier)
• Can resign anytime to President
• Salary equivalent to Supreme Court Judge (~₹2.50 lakh/month)
• Conditions cannot be varied to disadvantage after appointment
Removal Safeguards
• CEC: Removed like Supreme Court Judge – impeachment by Parliament (Article 124(4))
• Other ECs: Removed only on recommendation of CEC (Article 324(5))
• Provides substantial independence from executive interference
Powers & Functions
Delimitation & Electoral Rolls
Determining territorial constituencies; preparation, revision & updating of electoral rolls
Election Scheduling
Notification of dates & scrutiny of nominations; establishing election timetable
Party Recognition
Granting national/state party status & allotting symbols; settling splits/mergers
Model Code of Conduct
Formulation & enforcement of MCC; regulating campaigns & ensuring fair play
Media Regulation
Roster for party broadcasts on AIR/Doordarshan; monitoring political advertisements
Poll Cancellation
Cancelling elections in cases of booth capturing, violence, or rigging

ARTICLE 325

No Discrimination in Electoral Rolls - Universal Franchise Principle
Constitutional Text & Meaning
Text: "No person shall be ineligible for inclusion in any electoral roll... on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or any of them."
One General Electoral Roll
Single integrated electoral roll for every territorial constituency - no separate rolls based on identity markers
Historical Context
Direct response to the communal electorate system under British colonial rule which divided electorates along religious lines
Secular Foundation
Electoral process completely divorced from identity politics; foundation of India's secular democratic character
Exceptions & Limitations
Special Provisions (Historical)
• Anglo-Indian community: Nominated members in Parliament & State Legislatures (Abolished by 104th Amendment, 2019)
• Scheduled Castes/Tribes: Reserved constituencies but using the same general electoral roll
Important Distinction
Note: Discrimination is prohibited in roll preparation, not in reservation of seats. Article 330 provides for reservation of seats for SCs/STs in Parliament.

ARTICLE 326

Elections to House of People & Legislative Assemblies - Adult Suffrage
Adult Suffrage - The Great Equalizer
Constitutional Philosophy: "One Person, One Vote, One Value" - equality in voting power regardless of education, wealth, gender, or social status.
Historical Evolution
• 1950: Voting age = 21 years
• 1989: Reduced to 18 years by 61st Constitutional Amendment
• Rationale: Youth participation & harnessing demographic dividend
Eligibility Criteria
1. Citizenship: Must be citizen of India
2. Age: 18 years or above on qualifying date (1st January of year)
3. Ordinary Resident: Of constituency where registering
4. Not Disqualified: Under Constitution or law
Grounds for Disqualification
Unsound Mind
Declared by competent court under relevant laws
Criminal Conviction
For certain offenses (corrupt practices, electoral offenses) as per Representation of People Act, 1951
Office of Profit
Holding office of profit under Union or State government (with exceptions)
Other Disqualifications
Being disqualified by law (Representation of People Act, 1951) or under Tenth Schedule (anti-defection)

ARTICLE 327

Power of Parliament to Make Election Laws - Legislative Framework Authority
Constitutional Text & Scope
Text: "Subject to the provisions of this Constitution, Parliament may from time to time by law make provision with respect to all matters relating to elections..."

This article grants Parliament comprehensive power covering the entire electoral process, establishing its supremacy in election-related legislation.

ARTICLE 328

Power of State Legislature to Make Election Laws - Subsidiary Legislative Authority
Scope & Limitations
Text: "Subject to the provisions of this Constitution and... any law made by Parliament, the Legislature of a State may from time to time by law make provision with respect to all matters relating to elections to such Legislature."
Can Regulate
• Elections to State Legislature only (not local bodies)
• Procedural aspects not covered by Parliament
• Local adaptations of central laws
• Administrative arrangements for state elections
Cannot Regulate
• Parliament elections
• Presidential/Vice-Presidential elections
• Matters already covered by Parliament law
• Fundamental aspects of electoral process
Doctrine of Repugnancy
Under Article 254, if any provision of a State law relating to elections is repugnant to any provision of a Parliamentary law on the same subject, the Parliamentary law prevails and the State law becomes void to the extent of repugnancy.
Practical Examples
Bye-election Procedures
State laws governing procedures for bye-elections to state assemblies when vacancies occur
Local Roll Maintenance
Mechanisms for local electoral roll maintenance and voter registration drives
Remote Area Arrangements
Special polling station arrangements in remote, tribal, or inaccessible areas

ARTICLE 329

Bar to Interference by Courts - Judicial Non-Interference Principle
Detailed Constitutional Provisions

Article 329(a) - Protection of Delimitation

Prevents the validity of any law relating to the delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats from being called into question in any court.

This clause provides finality to delimitation exercises conducted by the Delimitation Commission, preventing continuous litigation that could disrupt electoral processes.

Article 329(b) - Election Petition Requirement

States that no election to Parliament or a State Legislature shall be called into question except by an election petition presented to such authority and in such manner as may be provided for by or under any law made by the appropriate legislature.

This establishes the exclusive mechanism for challenging election results, preventing parallel judicial proceedings that could create uncertainty.

Election Petition Mechanism
Forum
• High Court: For state legislature elections
• Supreme Court: For Parliament elections
Time Limit
Must be filed within 45 days of result declaration (Section 81 of RPA, 1951)
Grounds
• Corrupt practices
• Disqualification of candidate
• Irregularities affecting result
• Improper acceptance/rejection of nominations
Appeal
To Supreme Court (for High Court decisions) as per Section 116A of RPA, 1951
Constitutional Rationale
Prevent Election Delays
Avoid indefinite delays in election process through continuous litigation
Protect Electoral Process
Shield electoral process from frivolous challenges that could undermine democratic mandate
Balance Judicial Review
Balance between judicial review & electoral finality; provides structured challenge mechanism
Judicial Interpretation: The Supreme Court has held that Article 329(b) creates a complete bar on judicial interference in electoral matters except through election petitions. This ensures that election disputes are resolved through specialized tribunals rather than regular courts, maintaining the sanctity and timeliness of the electoral process.

India's Democratic Architecture
Articles 324-329 collectively establish India as the world's largest functioning democracy with institutional safeguards for free, fair, and periodic elections. The Election Commission stands as a constitutional sentinel protecting electoral integrity while balancing federal principles, judicial oversight, and technological evolution in its quest for perfect democratic expression. These constitutional provisions embody the vision of India's founding fathers for a robust, inclusive, and vibrant democracy where every citizen's vote carries equal weight and meaning
