🧭 Act I · The Constitutional Compass

A Union of States — strong, plural and never without limits.

Article 1 calls India a “Union of States”. Dr Ambedkar explained that the federation is not the result of an agreement among States and that no State may secede — yet States are constitutionally recognised, elected governments with real fields of power. Federalism is part of the basic structure (Kesavananda Bharati; S. R. Bommai). Parliament may re-draw State boundaries under Article 3; the State legislature’s views are sought but are not binding.

📜

Legislative · Part XI, Ch. I

Articles 245–255 (with 246A): territory, subject lists, special Parliamentary routes and conflict rules.

⚙️

Administrative · Part XI, Ch. II

Articles 256–263 (with 258A): compliance, directions, entrustment, full faith and credit, river disputes and the Inter-State Council.

💰

Financial · Part XII

Articles 264–293: taxation, divisible pool, grants, Finance Commission, GST Council, immunities and borrowing.

⚖️

Review & dialogue

Courts police boundaries (Arts. 131, 226, 32); councils and commissions manage cooperation. Neither replaces the other.

🗂️ The Seventh Schedule — read the entries, not the totals

I

🇮🇳 Union List

Original 97 entries; popularly cited today as about 100. Defence (1), armed forces (2), atomic energy (6), foreign affairs (10), railways (22), currency (36), banking (45), residuary (97).

II

🌳 State List

Original 66; popularly cited as 61. Public order (1), police (2), local government (5), public health (6), agriculture (14), land (18), alcohol excise (51).

III

🤝 Concurrent List

Original 47; popularly cited as 52. Criminal law (1), criminal procedure (2), marriage (5), forests (17A), planning (20), education (25), electricity (38).

🚨 Count with care: “100 + 61 + 52 = 213” is a textbook approximation. Lettered entries and omissions change the count, so verify before claiming exact totals. An entry is a legislative field, not an isolated “subject”.

🕰️ The federal journey — not one straight line

1950–1967 · INTEGRATION & PLANNING

Consolidation, dominant-party politics, the Planning Commission, linguistic reorganisation (States Reorganisation Act, 1956).

1967–1989 · REGIONAL ASSERTION

Opposition-ruled States, autonomy demands (Rajamannar, Anandpur Sahib), contested Article 356 use, Emergency centralisation (1975–77).

1989–2014 · COALITIONS & DECENTRALISATION

Inter-State Council (1990), 73rd/74th Amendments, Bommai (1994), liberalisation, bargaining federalism.

2014 ONWARD · NEW INSTITUTIONS, OLD TENSIONS

NITI Aayog (2015); GST and the GST Council (101st Amendment, 2016); pandemic governance; borrowing, Governor and agency disputes.

🧩 Asymmetry inside one Constitution

Fifth and Sixth Schedule areas, the Article 371 series and special Union Territory arrangements (for example Article 239AA for Delhi) show that India is a differentiated federation. Asymmetry is not sovereignty and gives no right to secede; each provision must be read on its own terms.

ART.245🌍 Territorial extent of lawsIndia · State · extra-territorial operation · territorial nexus
THE RULE

Parliament may legislate for the whole or any part of India; a State Legislature for the whole or any part of its State. A Parliamentary law is not invalid merely because it operates extra-territorially.

  • Parliament: external operation needs a real connection with India (GVK Industries v. ITO, 2011) — not a licence to regulate unrelated foreign matters.
  • State: may reach persons or activities outside the State only on a real and relevant territorial nexus (State of Bombay v. R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala, 1957).
🚨 Do not write “absolute Parliamentary sovereignty”. All legislatures are bound by the Constitution.
🧠 245 = WHERE?Parliament: India + connected external reach · State: its territory + real nexus.
ART.246🗂️ Subject-matter distributionUnion · State · Concurrent · territories outside any State
  • 246(1): Parliament has exclusive power over List I, “notwithstanding” clauses (2) and (3).
  • 246(2): Parliament and State Legislatures both may legislate on List III, with Union priority in conflict.
  • 246(3): States have exclusive power over List II, subject to clauses (1) and (2).
  • 246(4): Parliament may legislate on any matter for territory not included in a State, even a State List matter.

🔄 The 42nd Amendment (1976)

Five subjects moved from the State List to the Concurrent List: Education · Forests · Wild animals/birds · Weights & measures · Administration of justice (courts other than SC/HC). It also inserted a new entry on population control and family planning (20A).

🧠 EFWWA“Educated Forest Wardens Weigh Appeals.” · Priority ladder: Union > Concurrent > State.
ART.246A🏷️ Special GST competence101st Amendment, 2016 · its own distribution rule

Notwithstanding Articles 246 and 254, Parliament and State Legislatures may legislate on GST. Parliament has exclusive power over GST on inter-State supply. Petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, motor spirit, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel come into GST only from a date the Council recommends; alcohol for human consumption stays outside GST (Art. 366(12A)).

ART.247⚖️ Additional courtsFor better administration of Parliamentary laws

Parliament may by law establish additional courts for better administration of laws made by Parliament, or existing laws on a Union List matter.

🚨 Not every tribunal or special court is an Article 247 institution. Check each body’s own statute and constitutional basis.
ART.248🧪 Residuary powersEntry 97, List I · subject to Article 246A

Parliament has exclusive power over any matter not in the Concurrent or State List, including a tax not mentioned in either. A new technology is not automatically “residuary”: first test the existing entries by pith and substance.

🧠 WHO HOLDS THE RESIDUE?India = Centre (like Canada) · USA = States (10th Amendment) · Australia = States (Commonwealth powers are enumerated).

ART.250🚨 Legislation during a National EmergencyArticle 352 trigger · six-month tail

While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, Parliament may legislate on State List matters. Such a law ceases six months after the Proclamation ends (past acts saved).

  • Art. 352 grounds: war, external aggression or armed rebellion (the 44th Amendment replaced “internal disturbance”). The Cabinet’s written decision is needed and both Houses must approve within one month.
  • Art. 359: Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended. India has had three National Emergencies (1962, 1971, 1975).
🚨 Article 250 does not impose President’s Rule (Art. 356) and does not dissolve State legislatures.
ART.251🔀 Inconsistency under 249 and 250Temporary inoperativeness of conflicting State provisions

States keep their legislative power, but a conflicting State provision is inoperative to the extent of repugnancy for as long as the Parliamentary law continues to have effect — whether the Parliamentary law is earlier or later. It revives afterwards.

ART.253🌐 Implementing international agreementsTreaty-making ≠ domestic implementation

Parliament may legislate to implement any treaty, agreement or convention, or decision of an international conference or body, even on a State List subject.

  • Examples: Geneva Conventions Act, 1960; Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (after Stockholm).
  • Maganbhai Ishwarbhai Patel (1969): ascertaining a boundary differs from ceding territory (which needs an amendment). Jolly George Varghese (1980): treaties do not override domestic law. Vishaka (1997): international norms consistent with fundamental rights can guide interpretation.
🚨 Article 253 cannot be used against the Constitution or Fundamental Rights. Signing, ratifying and implementing are different legal events.
ART.254⚖️ Repugnancy in the Concurrent fieldTwo clauses · Presidential-assent route

254(1): if a State law is repugnant to a Parliamentary law (earlier or later) or an existing law on a Concurrent matter, the Union law prevails and the State law is void to the extent of repugnancy.

254(2): a State law on a Concurrent matter that is repugnant to an earlier Union law prevails in that State if reserved for the President and assented to. The proviso lets Parliament later add to, amend, vary or repeal that State law.

↔ Direct conflict

Can both laws be obeyed together? Genuinely inconsistent commands signal repugnancy (M. Karunanidhi, 1979).

🧩 Occupied field

Did Parliament intend an exhaustive code? (Zaverbhai, 1954). Intention must be shown, not presumed.

🔎 Same field and aspect

Identify the common field first; distinct aspects can co-exist (Hoechst, 1983). Assent needs real consideration (Kaiser-I-Hind, 2002).

🚨 Article 254 has two clauses (the second has a proviso), not three. Mere overlap is not repugnancy, and assent cannot cure lack of competence or a rights violation.
ART.255🩹 Recommendations and sanctions as procedureA limited curative provision

An Act is not invalid merely because a required recommendation or previous sanction was not given, if the specified assent was given: for a missing Governor’s recommendation, the assent of the Governor or the President; for a missing President’s recommendation, the assent of the President.

🧠 255Missing recommendation + proper assent = procedural cure. Missing competence ≠ cured.

🧩 The interpretive toolkit

🪨

Pith and substance

Identify the true nature of the law; incidental overlap with another list does not invalidate it (Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee, 1947).

🎭

Colourable legislation

A legislature cannot do indirectly what it cannot do directly (K. C. Gajapati Narayan Deo, 1953).

🎼

Harmonious construction

Read entries together and give each effect before assuming conflict.

🔗

Ancillary powers

Power on a subject includes reasonably necessary incidental matters.

🌐

Territorial nexus

A real, relevant connection lets a State reach beyond its borders in limited ways.

🔭

Aspect theory

Different legal aspects of one activity may fall under different fields; it does not license duplicate taxation without competence.

⚙️ Act III · From Power to Performance

A law needs a federation in action.

Executive power broadly follows legislative competence (Arts. 73 and 162), but Articles 256–263 add duties, directions, entrustment and cooperative machinery. Union legislation does not automatically move the whole executive field to the Union.

ART.256📋 Compliance with lawsState duty · purpose-linked Union directions

Every State must exercise executive power so as to ensure compliance with Parliamentary laws and applicable existing laws. The Union may give directions necessary for that purpose.

⚖️ Directions must serve compliance — this is not general supervision of State policy. Non-compliance with a lawful direction can lead to Article 365, but never automatically to President’s Rule.

📚 From 1 July 2024 the BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act for new matters; older cases still cite the earlier statutes.

ART.257🛣️ Non-impediment, communications, railwaysFour clauses · cost adjustment
  • (1) State power must not impede or prejudice Union executive power; the Union may direct accordingly.
  • (2) Directions on construction and maintenance of communications declared of national or military importance.
  • (3) Directions on protecting railways in the State.
  • (4) Extra costs under (2)/(3) are paid as agreed, or fixed by an arbitrator appointed by the Chief Justice of India.

OMITTEDFormer Article 257A (Union forces in States) was inserted by the 42nd and omitted by the 44th Amendment — never cite it as operative.

🧠 N-C-R-CNon-impediment · Communications · Railways · Costs.
ARTS.258·258A🔄 Entrustment of functions, both waysConsent route · statutory route · costs
  • 258(1) The President, with the State Government’s consent, may entrust Union executive functions to the State or its officers.
  • 258(2) A Parliamentary law may itself confer powers and impose duties on State officers — no consent clause.
  • 258(3) Additional costs are agreed or fixed by a CJI-appointed arbitrator.
  • 258A (7th Amendment, 1956): the Governor, with the Government of India’s consent, may entrust State functions to the Union.

ART.259⚔️ Armed forces in Part B StatesHistorical · repealed in 1956

REPEALEDConcerned armed forces in former Part B States; repealed by the 7th Amendment (1956). Defence is Union List Entry 1; State police remains a State field.

ART.260🌍 Functions in a territory outside IndiaBy agreement · foreign-jurisdiction law

The Government of India may, by agreement with the government of a territory outside India, undertake executive, legislative or judicial functions vested in that government, subject to the law on foreign jurisdiction (Foreign Jurisdiction Act, 1947).

🚨 Not every diplomatic mission or peacekeeping deployment is an Article 260 arrangement. Maganbhai concerns boundary implementation, not general validation of executive agreements.
ART.261📄 Full faith and creditPublic acts · records · judicial proceedings

Full faith and credit must be given throughout India to public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and every State. Parliament may prescribe how they are proved and their effect. Final civil judgments are executable anywhere in India according to law.

🧠 261Recognise → Prove → Give effect → Execute (civil judgments, lawfully).

ART.263🤝 The Inter-State CouncilEnabling article · duties set by Presidential order

If public interest requires, the President may establish a Council to (a) inquire into and advise on inter-State disputes, (b) investigate and discuss common-interest subjects, or (c) recommend better policy coordination.

  • The present Council was set up by Presidential order on 28 May 1990 after the Sarkaria Commission’s recommendation.
  • Composition (as commonly stated): PM (Chair); Chief Ministers of States and of UTs with legislatures; administrators of other UTs; six Union Cabinet Ministers nominated by the PM. A Standing Committee is chaired by the Union Home Minister.
  • Its role is consultative. Meeting frequency comes from the order and practice, not the article, and actual meetings have been far fewer than envisaged.

💰 Act IV · The Currency of the Compact

Responsibilities are distributed. Resources must follow.

The Union generally has the stronger revenue base while States carry heavy duties in health, education, policing and local development. Vertical imbalance is between levels of government; horizontal imbalance is among States.

ART.268📜 Union levy, State collectionSpecified stamp duties · current post-GST position

Stamp duties specified in the Union List (for example on bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes) are levied by the Union but collected by States (by the Union in Union Territories); proceeds go to the States and not to the Consolidated Fund of India.

🧯 The old “medicinal and toilet preparations” limb was removed by the 101st Amendment. Article 268A (service tax, inserted in 2003) was omitted in 2016. State stamp duty on property transfers is a separate State power.
ARTS.269·269A🔄 Assigned taxes and Inter-State GSTDo not confuse the two
  • 269: specified inter-State taxes are levied and collected by the Union and assigned to States on Parliament’s principles — its scope is much narrower in the GST era.
  • 269A: GST on inter-State supply (and imports, treated as inter-State) is levied and collected by the Union and apportioned between Union and States as Parliament provides on the GST Council’s recommendation. The Constitution fixes no “50:50” ratio.
🧠 269 vs 269A269 = old assigned taxes · 269A = IGST apportioned.
ART.270🤝 The divisible poolNet proceeds shared between Union and States

Union taxes are distributed on the President’s prescription after Finance Commission recommendations, excluding taxes under 268, 269 and 269A, surcharges (271) and cesses for specific purposes.

📅 Dated example: the 14th FC recommended 42%; the 15th FC recommended 41% of the divisible pool (the 1% adjustment reflected the new UTs of J&K and Ladakh). Verify the 16th Commission’s award for 2026–31.
🚨 “41% of all Union revenue” is wrong. The base is the divisible pool. Because cesses and surcharges sit outside it, the effective share of gross tax revenue is lower than the headline percentage.
ART.271➕ Surcharges for Union purposesUnion keeps the proceeds · GST excepted

Parliament may impose surcharges on specified duties and taxes for Union purposes, notwithstanding 269 and 270 (not on GST under 246A). Proceeds form part of the Consolidated Fund of India and are not shared.

🧠 S-A-A-P-S268 Stamp · 269 Assigned · 269A Apportioned · 270 Pool · 271 Surcharge.
ARTS.272–274🗃️ Omitted distribution, jute grants, State-interest tax Bills272 omitted · 273 transitional · 274 live
  • OMITTED272 (Union duties that may be distributed) was omitted by the 80th Amendment (2000, effective 1 April 1996). It was not a “railway fare” article.
  • 273: grants in lieu of jute export duty to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal — a time-limited transitional arrangement (ten years from commencement), not an ongoing grant.
  • 274: specified tax Bills in which States are interested cannot be introduced in Parliament without the President’s recommendation — a pre-introduction safeguard.
ART.275🤲 Statutory grants-in-aidCharged on the Consolidated Fund of India

Parliament may grant sums to States it determines need assistance (different sums for different States). Provisos provide special sums for Scheduled Tribe welfare and Scheduled Area administration, with specified Assam provisions.

🧠 275 vs 282275 = structured grants-in-aid to States in need · 282 = public-purpose grants even outside the grantor’s legislative field.
ARTS.276–278💼 Profession tax · saved levies · repealed 278₹2,500 ceiling
  • 276: States and local authorities may tax professions, trades, callings and employments up to ₹2,500 per person per year (raised from ₹250 by the 60th Amendment, 1988); this does not curtail the Union’s income-tax power.
  • 277: certain pre-Constitution State/local levies continue until Parliament provides otherwise.
  • REPEALED278: financial agreements with Part B States (7th Amendment, 1956).
ART.279🧮 Net proceeds and certificationCollection costs deducted · CAG certifies

“Net proceeds” means proceeds minus the cost of collection. The Comptroller and Auditor-General ascertains and certifies the figure; the certificate is final.

🚨 Not the President, not the Finance Commission — the CAG. The Constitution fixes no “2–3% collection charge”.
ART.279A🏷️ The GST CouncilComposition · weighted voting · recommendations · disputes

Members: Union Finance Minister (Chair); Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance; the Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation (or another Minister) from each State and legislature-bearing UT. The State members choose a Vice-Chair. It recommends on taxes subsumed, exemptions, model laws, rates, thresholds, place of supply, special rates for calamities, and the date for GST on specified petroleum products. A mechanism for resolving specified disputes is required (279A(11)).

Constituted by the President; qualifications are set by the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951. The Chair has public-affairs experience; members come from: High-Court-judge qualification; government finance and accounts; financial and administrative experience; economics.

Disasters: the Disaster Management Act, 2005 sets up NDMA (chaired by the PM), SDMAs (chaired by CMs) and district authorities — distinct from a National Emergency.

GOVERNOR👑Governors and Bill assentArts. 153–167, 200–201, 213, 355–356, 361

The Governor is the constitutional head of a State, appointed by the President, acting generally on the advice of the State ministry except where discretion is allowed. Distinguish aid and advice, government formation, floor tests and reservation of Bills. Article 361’s personal immunity does not make official action immune from review.

Article 200 covers assent, withholding, return of a non-Money Bill and reservation; Article 201 covers reserved Bills. Judicial directions on timelines (Punjab, 2023; Tamil Nadu, 2025) and the follow-on Presidential reference are update-sensitive — read the latest opinion before stating a final position.

🔭 Act VII · The Unfinished Partnership

A stronger federation is not a weaker Union.

India needs national capacity for defence, macro-stability and common markets, alongside real State discretion. The test is whether power, responsibility, finance and accountability are aligned. Separate existing law, documented practice and proposals.

💰

Fiscal balance

Predictable transfers aid planning; equalisation addresses unequal capacity. Cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool cut the effective share below the headline percentage. Excess conditionality weakens State ownership; no monitoring weakens accountability.

👑

Governors and trust

Transparent appointments, neutrality and timely, reasoned decisions reduce friction. Distinguish enforceable duties from conventions and from proposals needing amendment.

💧

Water, ecology, climate

Reliable basin data, ecological flows and drought protocols matter as much as adjudicating historic allocations. Negotiate before scarcity reaches the courtroom.

🛡️

Security with oversight

Cooperation against organised crime, terrorism and cyber threats must stay statute-specific and rights-respecting; clear protocols beat improvisation.

🧬

Digital and health federalism

National data systems help portability but are implemented by institutions of unequal capacity. Technology does not erase constitutional competence.

🏘️

The missing third tier

Functions, funds and functionaries must reach Panchayats and Municipalities; autonomy arguments are incomplete if States centralise within their own territory.

🛒 The economic federation: Articles 301–307

Part XIII protects freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse (301) subject to Articles 302–307; Article 304(b) requires Presidential sanction for the relevant State Bill. In Jindal Stainless (2016), taxation is not automatically a forbidden restriction — discriminatory taxation is what Article 304(a) targets.

🗳️ Representation questions

Delimitation, population-based distribution and fiscal equalisation are related but legally distinct. The existing freeze on seat readjustment is tied to the first census taken after 2026; it does not mean redistribution happens automatically on a fixed date. Label proposals such as simultaneous elections by their actual legislative status.

🧯 Precision repairs: do not carry old errors forward

Incorrect shortcut → accurate constitutional position
Unsafe statementAccurate replacement
“Parliament has absolute sovereignty.” The Constitution is supreme. Competence, Fundamental Rights and other constitutional limits bind Parliament.
“A newly emerging technology automatically belongs to the residue.” First interpret existing legislative entries. Residuary competence is not merely a label for unfamiliar technology.
“Article 251 puts 249 laws above 250 laws.” It gives the relevant Parliamentary law priority over conflicting State provisions; it creates no such ranking between the two routes.
“Presidential assent under 254(2) saves every State law.” It addresses specified repugnancy on Concurrent matters; it does not cure lack of competence or a Fundamental Rights violation.
“A State can withdraw from an Article 252 Act by cancelling its resolution.” The operative Parliamentary Act is not undone by unilateral withdrawal of a resolution.
“Article 255 cures a defective constitutional amendment.” It is confined to specified recommendation or previous-sanction defects and the requisite assent. It does not cure missing Article 368 majorities or ratification.
“Every Union-to-State entrustment requires consent.” Article 258(1) requires State consent; Article 258(2) separately permits Parliamentary legislation imposing duties or conferring powers.
“Non-compliance with a Union direction automatically brings President’s Rule.” Article 365 is an enabling constitutional inference, not an automatic replacement for Article 356 safeguards.
“Article 269 is the IGST article.” IGST is specifically governed by Article 269A. Article 269 retains a different assignment framework.
“All GST revenue is excluded from Article 270 distribution.” Article 270(1A) and (1B) expressly bring specified GST proceeds into its distribution framework. Read these alongside Article 269A.
“The President certifies net proceeds.” The CAG certifies under Article 279.
“Finance Commission members must serve five years.” The constitutional interval for constituting a Commission is distinct from member tenure and the period covered by an award.
“GST Council recommendations are binding legislation.” Recommendations are not legislation; operative statutes and lawful notifications bind taxpayers and authorities.
“Article 285 contains the same commercial exception as Article 289.” The provisions are differently worded. Do not import Article 289(2)’s trade/business qualification into Article 285.

🎯 Scenario theatre: decide before revealing

🏛️ Rajya Sabha has 180 members present. Twenty abstain. What minimum support satisfies Article 249?

107 votes. There are 160 members present and voting. Two-thirds is 106⅔, so at least 107 affirmative votes are required. Quorum and other procedural requirements must separately be satisfied.

🕰️ An Article 249 resolution expires. Does the resulting law vanish that day?

No. Article 249(3) provides a six-month tail, to the extent that the law depends on the resolution for competence. Earlier things done or omitted are saved.

🤝 Two bicameral States request Article 252 legislation through their Assemblies alone. Is that sufficient?

No. The resolutions must be passed by all Houses of the participating State Legislatures. A later adopting State must likewise follow the constitutionally prescribed House-or-Houses process.

⚖️ A State law has different licensing requirements from a Union law. Is repugnancy automatic?

No. First establish the relevant legislative fields and actual inconsistency. Different requirements can sometimes be complied with together. A genuine conflict or demonstrated exhaustive Parliamentary occupation requires careful analysis.

🌐 India signs a treaty. Does every treaty provision automatically displace domestic legislation?

No. Treaty-making and domestic implementation are distinct. Legislation is needed where implementation requires a change to domestic law; existing constitutional powers may implement some obligations without new legislation. The Constitution remains supreme.

🔄 The Governor entrusts State executive functions to the Union. Which provision governs consent?

Article 258A. It requires the Government of India’s consent. This is executive entrustment, not a general transfer of State legislative power.

💧 A water-dispute statute excludes ordinary court jurisdiction. Is every Supreme Court proceeding therefore impossible?

No. Identify the nature of the proceeding and the governing case law. The Court has considered constitutional and implementation questions and entertained Article 136 appeals in the Cauvery litigation.

🏷️ The Union votes “no”, while every State vote cast supports a GST Council proposal. Does it pass?

No. State votes collectively contribute two-thirds, which is below the three-fourths threshold. Conversely, the Union’s one-third cannot pass a proposal without sufficient State support.

💰 A Finance Commission recommends a tax share of 41%. Is that 41% of every rupee collected by the Union?

No. The recommendation concerns the specified divisible pool, not all Union receipts or all gross taxes. Constitutional exclusions and the relevant GST distribution provisions matter.

💳 A State has an outstanding Union-guaranteed loan but no direct Union loan. Is Article 293(3) irrelevant?

No. Its consent condition also covers the constitutionally specified outstanding Union-guaranteed borrowing situation.

🏦 Does charged expenditure escape appropriation and audit?

No. “Charged” removes the vote on the item as a demand for grant. Budget presentation, appropriation and financial accountability remain.

👑 A Governor has personal immunity under Article 361. Are the Governor’s official decisions beyond review?

No. Personal immunity and judicial review of constitutional action are different questions. Appropriate proceedings can examine official action.

251. The conflicting State provision is temporarily inoperative to the extent of repugnancy.

6. Which special legislative route has no automatic sunset?

252. State-requested Parliamentary legislation continues subject to the prescribed amendment/repeal mechanism.

7. What does Article 254(2) require?

A State law on a Concurrent matter, repugnant to an earlier relevant Union or existing law, must be reserved for Presidential consideration and receive assent to prevail in that State, subject to Parliament’s later power.

8. Which article creates the State compliance obligation?

256.

9. What are Article 257’s four cues?

Non-impediment · communications · railway protection · additional costs.

The 7th Amendment, 1956.

11. Which provision concerns full faith and credit?

261. Public acts, records and judicial proceedings; final civil judgments are executable according to law.

12. When was the present Inter-State Council established?

28 May 1990, by Presidential order under Article 263.

No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.

14. Who certifies net proceeds?

The CAG, under Article 279.

15. What are the GST Council weights and threshold?

Union one-third; States collectively two-thirds; decision by not less than three-fourths of weighted votes of members present and voting. Quorum: half the total membership.

Union of India v. Mohit Minerals, 2022.

The Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951.

An explanatory memorandum on action taken, under Article 281.

282, for a public purpose, subject to other constitutional requirements.

292 and 293, respectively.

📚 Constitutional glossary

🗂️ Legislative entry

A broadly interpreted field of legislation, not a self-contained code or an independent source of unrestricted power.

⚖️ Repugnancy

A legally relevant inconsistency between competent laws in the applicable common field; mere thematic overlap is insufficient.

🔄 Entrustment

Assignment of executive functions under constitutional or statutory authority, distinct from redistribution of legislative competence.

🤝 Tax devolution

Distribution of shareable tax proceeds, distinct from discretionary grants, scheme expenditure and borrowing.

📊 Equalisation

Transfers designed to address differences in capacity and expenditure needs; it does not mean identical transfers to every State.

🏘️ Subsidiarity

A governance principle favouring decisions at the lowest effective level. It is an analytical guide, not a universal override of constitutional entries.
