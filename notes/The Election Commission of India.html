🎬 Chapter 01 · The Constitutional Atlas

One Part, Six Articles, One Electoral Republic

Part XV of the Constitution — Elections — contains Articles 324–329. (Art. 329A, inserted by the 39th Amendment in 1975, was omitted by the 44th Amendment in 1978.) Together the live articles create an administering institution, a common roll, adult suffrage, legislative competence at two levels, and a structured route for challenging elections.

𝟑𝟐𝟒

🏛️ The Commission

Superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President.

𝟑𝟐𝟓

🤝 One Common Roll

One general roll per territorial constituency; no exclusion “on grounds only of” religion, race, caste or sex.

𝟑𝟐𝟔

🗳️ Adult Suffrage

Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections rest on adult suffrage — Indian citizens aged 18+, unless lawfully disqualified.

𝟑𝟐𝟕

📚 Parliament’s Power

Parliament may legislate on rolls, delimitation and all matters connected with elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.

𝟑𝟐𝟖

🏢 State’s Gap-Filling

A State Legislature may legislate for its own House(s) only in so far as Parliament has not made provision.

𝟑𝟐𝟗

⚖️ Structured Challenge

Delimitation laws are shielded; elections are challenged only by election petition before the designated authority.

ARTICLE324🏛️ Superintendence, Direction & ControlComposition · tenure · removal · staff · plenary gap-filling

Article 324(1) vests the superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of elections to Parliament, the State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission. It is a permanent constitutional body, not a ministry department.

Overall oversight: planning, monitoring and coordinating electoral administration.

ARTICLE325🤝 One General Electoral RollNo identity-based exclusion or special-roll claim
“There shall be one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency… and no person shall be ineligible for inclusion in any such roll or claim to be included in any special electoral roll for any such constituency on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or any of them.”Article 325 (condensed)

🕰️ Decolonising the ballot

Colonial law fragmented the electorate: separate Muslim electorates (Morley–Minto, 1909), extension to other groups (Montagu–Chelmsford, 1919) and the Communal Award (1932, followed by the Poona Pact). Art. 325 closes that door: citizens appear on one territorial roll.

🗳️ Reserved seats ≠ separate electorates

SC/ST reservation (Arts. 330 and 332) reserves candidacy in specified constituencies. All eligible electors of that constituency vote from the same common roll.

🔍 “Grounds only of…” — exclusion for other lawful reasons (non-citizenship, unsound mind declared by a court, statutory disqualification) is not barred. Art. 325 also does not mean every person is automatically a voter: registration conditions still apply.
🕰️ Anglo-Indian nomination ended in January 2020 (104th Amendment) while SC/ST seat reservation was extended; it was never a separate electoral roll.
🧠 R-R-C-SReligion · Race · Caste · Sex — the four banned “sole grounds”.
Common roll + reserved seat can coexist.
ARTICLE326🗳️ Adult SuffrageLok Sabha & State Assemblies · 18+ · lawful exclusions

Elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies are based on adult suffrage. Every citizen aged 18 or above on the qualifying date is entitled to be registered, unless disqualified under the Constitution or an appropriate law on the ground of non-residence, unsoundness of mind, crime, or corrupt or illegal practice.

🎂 21 → 18

The 61st Amendment Act, 1988 lowered the voting age from 21 to 18; it took effect on 28 March 1989. The earlier 21-year rule governed the 1951–52 elections.

🚫 Five constitutional grounds

  1. Non-residence
  2. Unsoundness of mind
  3. Crime
  4. Corrupt practice
  5. Illegal practice
📅 Four qualifying dates. Since the 2021 electoral-law amendment, eligibility can be reckoned on 1 January, 1 April, 1 July and 1 October. “Only 1 January” is outdated.
⚖️ Nature of the right. The Court has described the right to vote as a statutory right (Ponnuswami; Jyoti Basu; Kuldip Nayar), while PUCL (2003) treated the freedom of voting — the act of expressing a choice — as a facet of free expression under Art. 19(1)(a). The voter’s right to know a candidate’s background is also grounded in Art. 19(1)(a) (ADR, 2002).
🚨 Voter ≠ candidate. Office of profit, anti-defection and conviction-based disqualification belong to the candidate/member framework, not to ordinary voter registration. Art. 326 does not create direct popular voting for the Rajya Sabha, Councils, President or Vice-President.
🧠 N-U-C-C-I & the calendarNon-residence · Unsound mind · Crime · Corrupt practice · Illegal practice.
Qualifying dates JAN-APR-JUL-OCT → every third month.
Formula: Citizen + 18 + registered + not disqualified.
ARTICLE327📚 Parliament’s Power over Election LawRolls · delimitation · due constitution of Houses

Subject to the Constitution, Parliament may legislate on all matters relating to, or in connection with, elections to either House of Parliament and to the House(s) of a State Legislature, including the preparation of electoral rolls, the delimitation of constituencies and all matters necessary for the due constitution of those Houses.

ARTICLE328🏢 State Legislature’s Election LawOnly where Parliament has not provided

Subject to the Constitution, a State Legislature may legislate on elections to its own House or Houses — including rolls and matters necessary for their due constitution — “in so far as provision in that behalf is not made by Parliament”.

State may…State may not…
Fill genuine gaps for its own Assembly/Council electionsLegislate for Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, President or Vice-President elections
Legislate where no parliamentary provision existsRewrite nomination, counting, roll or petition rules already covered by RPA 1950/1951
🚨 Wrong lens: Art. 328 carries its own built-in limit; do not explain it through Art. 254 repugnancy (a Concurrent List doctrine). Also, Art. 328 does not govern Panchayat or municipal elections — Arts. 243K and 243ZA do. In practice the field is largely occupied by parliamentary law, so the operative space is narrow.

ARTICLE329⚖️ Bar to Interference by CourtsDelimitation shield · election petitions · continuity
LimbRuleQualification
329(a)The validity of any law relating to the delimitation of constituencies or allotment of seats, made or purporting to be made under Art. 327 or 328, cannot be questioned in any court.Delimitation orders published in the Gazette carry the force of law (Meghraj Kothari, 1967). Don’t stretch it to every boundary-related executive act.
329(b)No election to Parliament or a State Legislature can be called in question except by an election petition to the authority and in the manner provided by the appropriate legislature’s law.Aim: channel challenges and avoid mid-process litigation; not abolish judicial role.

🏛️ Forum

Election petitions on both parliamentary and State-legislature elections are tried by the High Court (RPA 1951 s.80A). Presidential/Vice-Presidential disputes go to the Supreme Court (Art. 71).

⏱️ Limitation

45 days from, but not including, the date of election of the returned candidate (s.81).

📋 Grounds

Section 100: disqualification, corrupt practice, improper acceptance/rejection of nomination, non-compliance — several grounds require a material effect on the result.

⬆️ Appeal

To the Supreme Court under s.116A, ordinarily within 30 days of the High Court’s order.

🔍 Ponnuswami (1952): “election” covers the whole process from notification to declaration; challenges should await the petition. Ashok Kumar (2000): obstructive or protracting intervention is barred, but limited orders that facilitate completion without questioning the election are not automatically barred. Timing, relief and effect matter.
🚨 Two errors to avoid: (1) “Lok Sabha election petitions start in the Supreme Court” — they start in the High Court. (2) “Art. 329 bans every court order during elections” — it channels, it does not abolish review.
🧠 329 = Boundaries + Ballot challenge(a) Delimitation shield · (b) Petition route.
80A → High Court · 81 → 45 days · 100 → grounds · 116A → Supreme Court, 30 days.
🏛️ Chapter 03 · People, Appointments & Independence

A Collective Body — With Practical Independence at Stake

The established structure is one Chief Election Commissioner and two Election Commissioners with equal voting weight. If unanimity fails, the majority decides; the CEC chairs but cannot overrule colleagues.

🕰️ How the Commission evolved

25 JANUARY 1950

ECI established; Sukumar Sen was the first CEC. (Celebrated as National Voters’ Day since 2011.)

OCT 1951 – FEB 1952

First general election under universal adult franchise, at unprecedented scale.

16 OCTOBER 1989

Two additional ECs appointed (S. S. Dhanoa and V. S. Seigell) — the first multi-member phase.

JANUARY 1990

The additional posts were abolished; the Commission reverted to a single member. The abolition was upheld in S. S. Dhanoa (1991).

1 OCTOBER 1993 →

Three-member body restored (ordinance, then the 1993 amendment): 1 CEC + 2 ECs; upheld in T. N. Seshan (1995).

2 MARCH 2023 → JANUARY 2024

Anoop Baranwal sets an interim selection panel; Parliament enacts the 2023 appointments law, brought into force in January 2024.

🛠️ Historical correction: T. Swaminathan was CEC from 1973 to 1977 — not a “temporary second Election Commissioner in 1974”. Do not link the 1989 additional ECs causally to the voting-age change.

🛡️ Removal: the constitutional asymmetry

Chief Election Commissioner

Removed only “in like manner and on like grounds” as a Supreme Court judge: proved misbehaviour or incapacity, by an address of each House supported by a majority of its total membership and at least two-thirds of members present and voting, followed by the President’s order.

Other ECs & Regional Commissioners

Cannot be removed except on the CEC’s recommendation. This is a safeguard, not a power of personal dismissal. The Constitution’s “no disadvantage” proviso is expressly about the CEC’s service conditions.

🧾 Different officers, different jobs

A Returning Officer runs the constituency election (nomination scrutiny, counting, declaration). An Electoral Registration Officer maintains the roll. A Booth Level Officer supports field-level roll work.

👁️ Control over borrowed staff

Officers engaged in election duty are deemed on deputation to the ECI for the period (RPA 1950 s.13CC; RPA 1951 s.28A), subject to its control, supervision and discipline. Observers watch roll work, expenditure and polling.

⚖️ Independence is not perfect insulation. Debated issues: executive weight in selection, unequal removal protection, financial autonomy and secretariat independence, reliance on borrowed staff, and post-retirement expectations. Advisory proposals include the Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990), the Law Commission’s 255th Report (2015) and Baranwal’s call for an independent secretariat.

⚙️ Chapter 04 · The Engine Room

Many Powers — Different Legal Sources

Good answers name the source of each power — Article 324, a statute, rules, the Symbols Order or a judgment — rather than attributing everything to Art. 324.

🛠️

Administrative

  • Supervising roll preparation, revision and updating
  • Election programme; formal notifications issued by the competent authority on ECI recommendation
  • Appointing observers; monitoring expenditure
  • Adjourning, countermanding or re-polling in cases such as booth capturing or destruction of ballot material (RPA 1951 ss.57, 58, 58A)
🧑‍⚖️

Advisory

  • Art. 103: opinion on a sitting MP’s disqualification under Art. 102(1); the President acts according to it
  • Art. 192: same for MLAs/MLCs, with the Governor
  • Art. 356(5)(b): certification relevant to extending President’s Rule beyond one year
🏛️

Quasi-judicial

  • Para 15, Symbols Order: disputes between rival groups of a recognised party (test of majority — Sadiq Ali)
  • s.10A RPA 1951: three-year disqualification for failing to lodge election expenses without good reason

📐 Delimitation: what the ECI does — and does not do

🗺️ Who draws the lines?

Under the Delimitation Act 2002 the Delimitation Commission is the principal boundary-maker: chaired by a serving or retired Supreme Court judge, with the CEC (or an EC nominated by the CEC) and the relevant State Election Commissioner as members. Some statutes assign specific delimitation tasks to the ECI, so “ECI never delimits” is also too absolute.

🔒 The post-2026 freeze

The 84th and 87th Amendments froze inter-State seat allocation until publication of the figures of the first census taken after 2026, and based readjustment on the 2001 census. Boundaries do not redraw themselves on 1 January 2026.

📜 Model Code of Conduct — the level-playing-field compact

⏱️ Scope and timing

The MCC applies from announcement of the election schedule until the process is complete. It evolved from a Kerala practice in 1960 into a code issued by the ECI over the following decades, covering rallies, polling-day conduct, observers, the party in power, official machinery and manifestos.

⚖️ Legal character

The MCC is not a single statute. Some conduct violates both the Code and independent law (RPA 1951, criminal law); the ECI enforces the Code through directions, notices and censure, not imprisonment.

🚨 MCC ≠ a complete criminal code; Art. 324 power ≠ power contrary to Parliament’s legislation.
🧠 Powers with limitsECI administers · courts try petitions · Speakers decide defection · delimitation follows its own law.
Powers triangle: A-A-Q = Administrative · Advisory · Quasi-judicial.
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Chapter 05 · The Citizen at the Centre

A Voter Is Not a Number — Registration Makes the Right Real

Art. 326 is the foundation; statutes operationalise registration and voting. A voter ID card alone does not prove that a name is on the current roll.

18

🎂 Age

Must be 18 on the applicable qualifying date; advance applications are allowed as per ECI instructions.

🇮🇳

Citizenship

Only Indian citizens may be registered (RPA 1950 s.16 disqualifies non-citizens).

🏠

Ordinary residence

Registration follows ordinary residence in the constituency. Owning property is not required; temporary absence does not end residence.

🚫 Registration disqualifications (s.16)

Non-citizenship; unsoundness of mind declared by a competent court; disqualification under law relating to corrupt practices or election offences. A diagnosis or disability alone is not the court declaration.

🔒 Custody and voting (s.62(5))

A person confined in prison or in police custody cannot vote, except a person under preventive detention. This voting bar is not identical to automatic deletion from the roll.

🚨 Vote once. Sections 17–18 RPA 1950 bar registration in more than one constituency or twice in the same constituency; multiple cards never mean multiple votes.

🌍 Overseas, service and special-category electors

  • Overseas Indian electors register under s.20A, usually at the passport address, and vote in person with the prescribed ID — not online.
  • Service voters: postal ballot / ETPBS for eligible categories; proxy voting only for the legally defined classified service voters.
  • Accessibility and home-voting facilities exist for specified senior-citizen and disability categories; thresholds and categories are update-sensitive.
  • Students and homeless persons: registration depends on lawfully established ordinary residence, not on owning a home.

🆔 EPIC vs roll

EPIC is an identification card; the electoral roll decides who may vote. Check your roll entry, constituency and polling station before election day. Carry EPIC or another identification document permitted by the ECI for that election.

🤝 Inclusion without exclusion

Accessible polling stations, ramps, signage, assistance permitted by law, large-print and braille information, and outreach help turn a formal franchise into meaningful participation. Disability is not a general disqualification from voting; only a competent-court declaration of unsound mind falls within the s.16 ground.

Home-voting and assisted-voting facilities for senior citizens above a notified age threshold and persons with benchmark disabilities have been used in recent elections. Thresholds and procedure are update-sensitive.

🗳️ NOTA — an expression, not a veto

The None of the Above option protects the secrecy of a voter’s decision not to support any contesting candidate. Following PUCL v. Union of India (2013), it was introduced on EVMs. NOTA votes are counted and recorded, but they are not votes for a candidate. In parliamentary and State-legislature elections NOTA does not invalidate the election, trigger an automatic fresh poll or disqualify candidates. The candidate with the highest number of candidate votes wins, even if NOTA receives more selections.

⚖️ Rajya Sabha distinction: in Shailesh Manubhai Parmar v. Election Commission of India (2018) the Supreme Court held that NOTA is not available in Rajya Sabha elections. Do not carry the direct-election rule into indirect elections.

🚩 Chapter 06 · Parties, Symbols & Political Finance

The Contest Needs Choices — And Transparent Rules

A party may be registered without being recognised. Registration is a statutory entry process; recognition is an electoral-performance classification. Part XV does not define political parties. Their legal architecture comes from the RPA 1951 (Part IVA, s.29A onwards), the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order 1968, and judicial decisions.

📝 Registration — Section 29A

An association or body of individual Indian citizens that wants registration as a political party applies to the ECI within 30 days of its formation. Its governing document must affirm faith and allegiance to the Constitution and to the principles of socialism, secularism and democracy, and undertake to uphold the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India. The ECI follows a prescribed application, publicity, objection and hearing process.

📌 Registered ≠ recognised

A registered unrecognised party has statutory registration but does not (or no longer does) meet the recognition thresholds. Recognition brings additional privileges: reserved-symbol arrangements, free broadcast time on public media, free copies of electoral rolls and a larger star-campaigner list.

⚖️ Deregistration is limited

In Indian National Congress (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare (2002) the Court held that the ECI has no general power to review and cancel a registration under s.29A. Narrow exceptions exist, such as registration obtained by fraud or a party being declared unlawful. Withdrawal of recognition and deregistration are different acts.

🔎 Use the operative Symbols Order. Recognition and its continuation are judged under the Order’s detailed conditions. These thresholds are study rules, not a live list of currently recognised parties.

🎨 Symbols and faction disputes

The Symbols Order separates reserved symbols (for recognised parties in their area) from free symbols. Registered unrecognised parties may seek a common-symbol concession on stated conditions, but registration alone never creates a permanently reserved symbol.

Under Paragraph 15, the ECI decides disputes between rival sections of a recognised party, each claiming to be that party. In Sadiq Ali v. ECI (1972) the Supreme Court upheld the framework. The Commission examines organisational support and legislative strength. A symbol decision is not a Tenth Schedule disqualification decision, which belongs to the Speaker or Chairman.

💰 Campaign finance — four separate accountability channels

🧾 Candidate expenditure

Sections 77–78 require a separate, correct account of election expenses and its lodging within 30 days of the returned candidate’s election. Rule 90 of the Conduct of Election Rules fixes constituency-category ceilings. Verify the currently notified figures.

⚖️ Failure to lodge

Under s.10A the ECI may disqualify a person for three years if the required account was not lodged in time and manner and there was no good reason. This is distinct from an election-petition allegation of excess spending.

🏢 Party contributions

Section 29B permits party contributions, subject to prohibitions such as government-company and foreign-source donations. Section 29C requires a report of contributions above ₹20,000 from one person in a financial year. That is a disclosure threshold, not a maximum donation limit. Tax law operates separately.

🔍 Monitoring ecosystem

Expenditure observers, accounting teams, flying squads, static surveillance teams and media monitoring support enforcement. A seizure or a complaint is not proof of guilt; evidence and due process still decide the outcome.

🚨 Candidate limit ≠ party spending limit. Expenditure “incurred or authorised” on a candidate does not escape scrutiny merely because another person paid for it.

🌟 Star campaigners and paid news

Under the s.77 framework a recognised party may notify up to 40 star campaigners and another registered party up to 20, within the prescribed period. The travel-expense exclusion is conditional. It is not blanket immunity for every rally, advertisement or candidate-specific item.

Paid news blurs the line between editorial content and advertising. ECI media-certification and monitoring committees examine suspect content, and the legal consequence depends on the facts and the attribution of expenditure. Do not describe “paid news” as one self-contained offence that covers every situation.

⚖️ Electoral bonds — the constitutional turning point

In Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (15 February 2024) a Constitution Bench struck down the Electoral Bond Scheme and the associated amendments. It held that anonymity infringed voters’ right to information under Art. 19(1)(a), and it invalidated the removal of the corporate-contribution cap as arbitrary. The Court also gave disclosure directions.

📌 Accurate exam language: “The Electoral Bond Scheme was struck down in 2024.” Do not write as if it is a current funding channel, and do not confuse the holding with proposals for a replacement system.

👤 Candidate eligibility — a separate constitutional lane

🎂 Minimum ages

Lok Sabha / Assembly: 25 years. Rajya Sabha / Legislative Council: 30 years (Arts. 84 and 173, supplemented by the RPA). The voting age of 18 is not the minimum age for legislative candidacy.

⚖️ Disqualifications

Arts. 102 and 191, the RPA 1951 and the Tenth Schedule address distinct grounds. Section 8 conviction-based disqualification has different categories; it is not one generic “two-year” rule.

In Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013) the Supreme Court struck down s.8(4), which had temporarily protected sitting legislators after conviction. Filing an appeal does not itself suspend disqualification. A stay of conviction has different consequences from a stay of sentence alone.

📂 Nomination, proposers, disclosures and deposits

A candidate set up by a recognised party ordinarily needs one proposer; other candidates need ten electors of the constituency as proposers. Candidates must file the prescribed affidavit on criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities and educational qualifications. In Resurgence India v. ECI (2013) the Court treated an affidavit with blank columns as defective. Voters’ right to know rests on ADR (2002) and Art. 19(1)(a).

🤫 Campaign restrictions and integrity

⏳ The 48-hour period

Section 126 restricts public meetings and election matter shown through cinematograph, television or similar apparatus during the 48 hours ending with the hour fixed for conclusion of poll in the polling area. Do not turn this into an unqualified ban on every communication everywhere.

📊 Exit polls

Section 126A restricts conducting and publishing exit-poll results during the notified period. Exit polls and pre-poll opinion surveys are different categories.

🚫 Corrupt practices

Section 123 lists bribery, undue influence, prohibited appeals and similar conduct. Section 100 governs when an election may be declared void. A corrupt-practice finding and a criminal prosecution follow different routes.

🛡️ Polling safeguards

Secrecy, identity checks, polling agents, mock polls, sealing, custody, observers and authorised re-polls support integrity.

🏆 Counting and electoral systems

Lok Sabha and Assembly constituencies use first-past-the-post: the highest vote-getter wins, not necessarily a majority of all votes cast. Counting is supervised by the Returning Officer, with candidates’ counting agents present and prescribed recount-application procedures.

🏛️ Indirect elections differ

Rajya Sabha members are elected by elected MLAs using proportional representation by the single transferable vote; the open-ballot rule for party MLAs was upheld in Kuldip Nayar (2006). The President and Vice-President are elected by distinct electoral colleges (Arts. 54–55 and 66).

🔁 Bye-elections

Section 151A generally requires vacancies to be filled within six months. Exceptions: the remainder of the term is under one year, or the ECI, in consultation with the Union government, certifies difficulty in holding the poll.

🔐 Chapter 08 · Technology & Verifiable Trust

Machines Assist Democracy — Procedures Make Them Accountable

Explain election technology through its legal authority, physical design, verification procedures and institutional safeguards. Avoid both absolute reassurance and unsupported allegations.

🔘

Balloting Unit

Sits inside the voting compartment and displays candidate names and symbols with selection buttons. It is one part of the EVM system, not an internet terminal.

🎛️

Control Unit

Kept with the Presiding Officer to enable voting and record electronic totals. ECI describes the machine as standalone, with a one-time-programmable chip and no ordinary wireless or internet connectivity.

🧾

VVPAT

The Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail prints a slip showing the selected candidate’s serial number, name and symbol. It is visible through a window for about seven seconds and then drops into a sealed compartment. The voter does not take it away.

📚 Law and judicial milestones

1984 · A. C. JOSE

ECI instructions cannot override existing statutory voting rules. Article 324 is not a substitute for legal authority.

1988 · SECTION 61A

Parliament inserted express statutory authority for voting machines in the RPA 1951.

2013 · SUBRAMANIAN SWAMY

The Court recognised the paper trail as essential to confidence in free and fair elections and directed phased introduction of VVPAT.

2019 · N. CHANDRABABU NAIDU

Mandatory random VVPAT verification increased to five polling stations per Assembly constituency or Assembly segment.

26 APRIL 2024 · ADR v. ECI

The Court rejected a return to paper ballots and universal slip-counting, while issuing additional safeguards on symbol-loading units and a procedure for specified candidates to request technical verification of machine memory.

🧭 Protocol update warning: later orders and ECI technical instructions may refine verification arrangements. Verify the latest procedure instead of treating a 2024 operational description as permanent.

🌐 Standalone ≠ online voting

ECI machines are designed without ordinary internet or wireless connectivity. Security also depends on manufacturing controls, firmware design, physical custody and transparent procedures.

🧠 Three separate questions

Design security asks how the system works. Operational integrity asks how equipment is handled. Public verifiability asks how stakeholders can check the process. A sound evaluation addresses all three.

🧬 New risks: deepfakes, privacy and remote voting

AI-generated impersonation, deceptive advertising, coordinated misinformation and misuse of personal data threaten informed voting. A balanced response combines quick correction, lawful platform action, disclosure, evidence preservation and proportionate enforcement, without treating all political criticism as misinformation.

Remote-voting prototypes must be distinguished from enacted voting entitlements. Eligibility, secrecy, auditability, consensus and legal authority all remain open questions. A demonstration is not implementation.

📂 Election-petition essentials

Under s.81, a candidate or an elector of the constituency may present an election petition within 45 days. Pleadings, necessary parties, security for costs, affidavits and corrupt-practice allegations have strict requirements. Section 100 separates grounds that operate directly from those requiring proof that the result, so far as it concerns the returned candidate, was materially affected. Improper rejection and improper acceptance of a nomination do not carry identical tests.

🏛️ Chronology of reform reports

1974–75 · TARKUNDE COMMITTEE

A citizens’ committee formed under J. P. Narayan’s initiative; an early reference on electoral reform and Commission independence.

1990 · DINESH GOSWAMI COMMITTEE

Examined election administration, expenditure and appointments; a major source on in-kind support and institutional reform.

1993 · VOHRA COMMITTEE

Highlighted the nexus between criminal networks, politicians and officials.

1998 · INDRAJIT GUPTA COMMITTEE

Supported State funding of elections in principle, with conditions and emphasis on support in kind. It created no enforceable entitlement.

1999 & 2015 · LAW COMMISSION (170th, 255th)

Addressed disqualification of persons facing serious charges, party regulation, finance and Commission independence. Recommendations are not enacted law.

2002 · NCRWC

The Constitution-review commission also dealt with electoral reform and appointments.

2024 · HIGH-LEVEL COMMITTEE ON SIMULTANEOUS ELECTIONS

Chaired by Ram Nath Kovind; recommended phased synchronisation requiring constitutional amendments. A related Constitution Amendment Bill was introduced and referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee. Verify current status.

🗓️ Simultaneous elections — analysis, not a slogan

Synchronisation raises questions about legislative terms, premature dissolution, confidence motions, federalism, logistics, accountability and ECI capacity. A committee report or bill is not operative law. Check the current amendment status.

👩 Women’s legislative representation

The 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 reserves one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly, including within SC/ST-reserved seats. Its operation is linked to the first census after commencement and the consequent delimitation exercise. Enactment is not immediate implementation.

🧩 Connect the articles: Arts. 330A, 332A and 334A belong in this discussion. Do not confuse women’s reservation with separate rolls or with reservation in the Rajya Sabha.
A credible Election Commission needs legal authority, institutional distance, transparent reasoning and public trust. None is a substitute for the others.
