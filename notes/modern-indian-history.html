🗺️

Old Trade Routes

  • India ↔ Europe traded since ancient times
  • Indian cotton, silk, spices were in high demand
  • Arab merchants controlled Asian trade routes
  • Italians (Venice/Genoa) controlled Mediterranean
  • Trade passed through Constantinople (Istanbul)
🏰

Ottoman Disruption (1453)

  • Ottoman Turks captured Constantinople in 1453
  • Old East-West trade routes fell under their control
  • Venice & Genoa merchants established monopoly
  • New European nation-states excluded from trade
  • West Europeans searched for new sea routes
⛵

European Response

  • Portugal & Spain sponsored merchant sea voyages
  • Scientific breakthroughs: marine compass invented
  • Long-distance ships developed
  • 1492: Columbus reached America (searching for India)
  • 1498: Vasco da Gama discovered Cape of Good Hope route

🇵🇹 Portuguese in India

1498
📅 20 May 1498
🚢 Vasco da Gama Arrives at Calicut
Arrived at Calicut (SW India's important seaport). King Zamorin welcomed him & granted trade privileges. After 3 months, returned with rich cargo — earning 60× the voyage cost!
1501
📅 1501 AD
🏭 Trading Factory at Cannore
Vasco da Gama returns, sets up trading factory at Cannore. Beginning of India-Portugal trade links. Calicut, Cannore & Cochin became important Portuguese trading centers.
1505
📅 1505 AD
👑 First Portuguese Governor: Francisco de Almeida
Famous for the Blue Water Policy (Cartaz System) — a naval trade license system. Ships without cartaz had their cargo seized. Maintained military ships alongside trading ships.
1510
📅 1510 AD
🏯 Albuquerque Captures Goa
Alfonso de Albuquerque (replaced Almeida in 1509) captured Goa from Bijapur's Sultan. Also captured Malacca & Ceylon. Built a fort at Calicut. Real Founder of Portuguese Power in India
1530
📅 1530 AD
🏙️ Capital Shifted to Goa
Governor Nino da Cunha shifted Indian capital from Cochin to Goa. In 1534 acquired Diu & Bassein from Bahadur Shah of Gujarat. 1559: Daman also acquired.
1831
📅 Portuguese Decline
📉 Decline of Portuguese Power
1631: Lost Hooghly to Mughal Emperor Shahjahan's noble Qasim Khan. 1661: King of Portugal gifted Bombay as dowry to England's Charles II. 1739: Marathas captured Salsette & Bassein. Goa, Diu & Daman remained until 1961!

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 English East India Company

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 English EIC — Key Facts

🏛️ Foundation

  • Founded 1599 by "Merchant Adventurers"
  • Full name: Governor & Company of Merchants Trading into the East Indies
  • Charter granted 31 Dec 1600 by Queen Elizabeth I
  • 15-year monopoly on Eastern trade

👑 Mughal Connection

  • 1608: Capt. William Hawkins sent to Jahangir's court
  • Portugese interference prevented factory at Surat
  • 1611: Defeated Portuguese at Battle of Swally Hole
  • 1613: Jahangir granted factory rights on West Coast
  • 1615: Sir Thomas Roe sent by James I → Royal Charter

🏯 Key Forts & Settlements

  • 1639: Leased Madras → Fort St. George built
  • 1668: Charles II gave Bombay on lease
  • 1698: Calcutta zamindari — Fort William (1700)
  • Agra, Ahmedabad, Orissa (Balasore, Hariharpur)

📜 Farrukhsiyar Farman (1717)

  • Called Company's "Magna Carta"
  • Duty-free trade in Bengal (₹3000 annual tax only)
  • Permission to buy land near Calcutta
  • Company's Bombay coins valid across Mughal empire

WarPeriodCauseResult
1st 1746–1748 Austrian War of Succession in Europe spreads to India Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle. Inconclusive
2nd 1750–1754 Succession disputes in Hyderabad & Carnatic French under Dupleix, English under Clive. French recalled Dupleix → English advantage
3rd 1758–1763 Seven Years' War in Europe (1756) 🏆 Battle of Wandiwash (Jan 22, 1760) — Sir Eyre Coote crushed French. Pondicherry surrendered Jan 16, 1761. Treaty of Paris (1763) ended French political ambition in India

🏆 Why English Won Over French — Key Reasons

  • Private company — less govt control, more enthusiasm & speed in decisions
  • Superior Navy — won all major sea battles, controlled Indian coasts
  • Controlled major cities — Calcutta, Bombay, Madras vs French only Pondicherry
  • Strong funding — Bengal revenues after Plassey boosted war chest
  • Better commanders — Sir Eyre Coote, Robert Clive, Major Stringer Lawrence
  • French neglected commerce for political ambition — ran out of funds
⚔️ Chapter 6 — Game-Changing Battles

🔥 Battle of Plassey (1757) & Buxar (1764)

"Plassey was not really a battle — it was a conspiracy. Fewer than 50 English soldiers died while Nawab lost ~500 men. Mir Jafar's treachery changed the course of Indian history." — From the transcript: Background to Battle of Plassey

🗺️ Background to Plassey

  • Bengal: richest province (today's Bangladesh + Bihar + Odisha)
  • 1717: Farrukhsiyar granted duty-free trade to EIC in Bengal
  • 1696–1706: Fort William (named after King William III) constructed
  • 1756: Nawab Alivardi Khan died → Sirajuddaula became Nawab
  • EIC misused "dastak" (free passes) for private trade
  • EIC fortified Fort William against Nawab's wishes
  • Nawab captured Fort William on 20 June 1756
  • Black Hole of Calcutta incident (allegedly in Fort William)

🗡️ The Conspiracy

  • Clive bribed Manikchand → recovered Calcutta (Jan 2, 1757)
  • Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757) — Nawab restored old privileges
  • EIC joined conspiracy to replace Sirajuddaula
  • Conspirators: Mir Jafar (Mir Bakshi), Manikchand, Amichand, Jagat Seth (biggest banker of Bengal), Khadim Khan
  • Plan: Replace Sirajuddaula with Mir Jafar as Nawab
  • EIC placed impossible demands on Nawab to provoke war

⚔️ Battle of Plassey — 23 June 1757

<50English soldiers killed
~500Nawab's soldiers killed
24Parganas given to EIC as reward
₹1.77 CrCompensation paid to EIC
₹2M+Clive's personal reward

Mir Jafar & Rai Durlabh's forces (major part of Nawab's army) didn't fight. Only Mir Madan & Mohanlal fought bravely. Mir Miran (Mir Jafar's son) killed Sirajuddaula on the way to Murshidabad.

1764
📅 22 October 1764
⚔️ Battle of Buxar — The Real Victory
Alliance against EIC: Mir Qasim (replaced Mir Jafar) + Shuja-ud-Daula (Nawab of Awadh) + Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II joined forces.

Why Mir Qasim rebelled: He was an able, efficient ruler who wanted freedom from foreign control. He abolished all inland duties to create level playing field. EIC took this as provocation.

Result: EIC (under Major Hector Munro) decisively defeated the combined forces. Most decisive battle establishing British supremacy
1765
📅 1765 AD
📜 Treaty of Allahabad & Diwani Rights
Clive (returned as Governor) signed Treaty of Allahabad. Diwani Rights (revenue collection) of Bengal, Bihar & Orissa granted to EIC. This was the foundation of British territorial empire in India.

🐎 Anglo-Maratha Wars

1st War1775–1782 Treaty of Salbai
2nd War1803–1805 Subsidiary Alliance
3rd War1817–1819 End of Maratha Empire
⚔️

1st Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782)

  • Ragoba (Raghunathrao) deposed Peshwa asked EIC for help
  • Treaty of Surat (1775) — Ragoba promised territories
  • Treaty of Salbai (1782) — Status quo maintained
  • EIC gained 20 years of peace with Marathas
🏛️

2nd Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805)

  • Peshwa Baji Rao II defeated by Holkar, sought British protection
  • Treaty of Bassein (1802) — Subsidiary Alliance signed
  • Scindia & Bhonsle opposed British → defeated by Wellesley
  • Treaty of Deogaon (Bhonsle) & Surji-Anjangaon (Scindia)
💥

3rd Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1819)

  • Pindaris (Maratha irregular cavalry) began looting EIC territories
  • Governor-General Hastings attacked Pindaris in 1817
  • Peshwa Baji Rao II, Holkar & Bhonsle II formed united front
  • British won → Peshwa pensioned off to Bithoor (Kanpur)
  • Maratha territories became Bombay Presidency

📉 Reasons for Maratha Defeat

  • Lack of unity among Maratha chiefs
  • No good relations with any other Indian state
  • Marathas collected "Chauth" (protection tax) — created enemies everywhere
  • Fell for British diplomatic traps (treaties) repeatedly
  • Excellent in guerrilla warfare but weak in open battle
  • British superior diplomacy isolated Marathas from Hyderabad, Mysore etc.

🔥 Chapter 9 — Great Uprising

🔥 Revolt of 1857 — First War of Independence

🔥 1857 REVOLT — CAUSES

⚔️ Military Causes

  • Greased cartridges (cow & pig fat) — Enfield Rifle
  • General Service Enlistment Act — cross seas
  • Low pay compared to British officers
  • Racial discrimination in promotions
  • Loss of foreign allowance (bhatta) in Sindh

💰 Economic Causes

  • Drain of wealth from India to Britain
  • Destruction of Indian handicrafts & textile industries
  • Permanent Settlement impoverished peasants
  • Heavy taxation policies by British
  • Moneylenders exploiting farmers

🏛️ Political Causes

  • Doctrine of Lapse — annexation of princely states
  • Annexation of Awadh (1856) shocked ruling class
  • Removal of Mughal Emperor's title
  • Displacement of traditional rulers
  • Nana Sahib's pension stopped

🙏 Socio-Religious Causes

  • Missionary activities — threat to religion
  • Religious Disabilities Act (1850)
  • Western education threatening culture
  • Sati abolition seen as religious interference
  • Widow Remarriage Act (1856)
Jan
📅 January 1857
🎯 The Spark — Greased Cartridges
New Enfield Rifles introduced. Cartridges rumoured to be greased with cow & pig fat. Soldiers had to bite cartridges before loading — unacceptable to both Hindus & Muslims.
29 Mar
📅 29 March 1857
🔫 Mangal Pandey — First Act of Rebellion
Sepoy Mangal Pandey of 34th Bengal Infantry at Barrackpore attacked British officers. Executed on April 8, 1857. First Martyr of 1857
10 May
📅 10 May 1857 — THE OUTBREAK
💥 Meerut Mutiny — Revolt Begins!
85 soldiers of 3rd Cavalry refused greased cartridges → sentenced to 10 years. Next day fellow soldiers rose in revolt, freed prisoners, killed British officers & marched to Delhi!
11 May
📅 11 May 1857
👑 Bahadur Shah Zafar — Symbolic Leader
Rebels reached Delhi, proclaimed last Mughal Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar as the leader of the revolt. Delhi became the center of the uprising.

✅ Why Revolt Succeeded Initially

  • Widespread civilian support especially in Awadh
  • Coordination between sepoys & local rulers
  • Spiritual symbol — Bahadur Shah Zafar united diverse groups
  • Surprise element caught British off-guard initially

❌ Why Revolt Ultimately Failed

  • No unified national leadership or coordination
  • Many Indian princes remained loyal to British (Scindia, Holkar)
  • British had superior communication (telegraph)
  • Gurkha, Sikh & Pathan soldiers sided with British
  • No ideological clarity — mostly negative (anti-British)

🏛️ After 1857 — Crown Rule Begins

  • Government of India Act 1858 — EIC abolished; Crown took direct control
  • Governor-General became Viceroy
  • Lord Canning became first Viceroy
  • Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858) — promised equal rights to all subjects regardless of race/religion
  • Indian Civil Services opened to Indians (though age limit manipulated)
  • Army reorganized — increased European component in Indian army

🕊️

Moderate Phase (1885–1905)

  • Constitutional methods: petitions, resolutions, delegations
  • Believed in British sense of justice
  • Dadabhai Naoroji: "Drain of Wealth" theory
  • Key figures: Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjee, Pherozeshah Mehta
  • Demands: Indianization of civil services, reduction of military expenditure
🔥

Extremist Phase (1905–1919)

  • Partition of Bengal (1905) ignited Swadeshi Movement
  • Lal-Bal-Pal — Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal
  • Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it!"
  • Boycott of British goods, national education, swadeshi
  • Surat Split (1907) — Moderates vs Extremists
  • Tilak arrested (1908), sentenced to 6 years in Burma

Gandhian Phase (1919–1947)

  • Mass participation — peasants, workers, women all joined
  • Ahimsa & Satyagraha as tools
  • Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)
  • Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)
  • Quit India Movement (1942)
  • India achieved independence on 15 August 1947
🕊️ Chapter 11

🕊️ Gandhi's Major Movements

🕊️ MAHATMA GANDHI — Key Movements

🌾 Champaran (1917)

  • Bihar's indigo farmers exploited under "tinkathia" system
  • Farmers forced to grow indigo on 3/20 of their land
  • Gandhi's first Satyagraha in India
  • Investigation ordered by Lt. Governor
  • Champaran Agrarian Act (1918) passed
  • Abolished tinkathia system

⚙️ Kheda (1918)

  • Gujarat farmers hit by famine & plague (1918)
  • Revenue demand despite crop failure
  • Gandhi supported no-tax campaign
  • Revenue remitted for poorest
  • Gandhi's power grew in Gujarat

🏭 Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)

  • Cotton mill workers demanded 50% wage hike
  • Millowners offered only 20%
  • Gandhi undertook fast → first fast in India
  • Mill owners agreed to 35% hike
  • Arbitration accepted

📜 Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)

  • Rowlatt Act: imprisonment without trial
  • Called "Black Act" by Indians
  • Hartal (shutdown) on 6 April 1919
  • Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)
  • General Dyer's inhuman firing: 379+ killed officially
  • Shook entire India's conscience

✊ Non-Cooperation (1920–1922)

  • Return of titles, honorary offices
  • Boycott of govt schools & colleges
  • Boycott of courts, elections, foreign cloth
  • Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922) — mob burned police station
  • Gandhi called off movement → criticized by Subhas, Nehru etc.
  • 22 policemen died in Chauri Chaura incident

🧂 Civil Disobedience / Salt March (1930)

  • Dandi March: 12 March – 6 April 1930 (240 miles)
  • Gandhi broke salt laws at Dandi coast (Gujarat)
  • Massive civil disobedience spread nationwide
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) — CDM suspended
  • Round Table Conferences (1930, 1931, 1932)
  • Poona Pact (1932) — Gandhi & Ambedkar

✊ Quit India Movement (1942) — "Do or Die"

  • August 8, 1942 — Bombay Session of INC passed Quit India Resolution
  • Gandhi gave the slogan "Do or Die"
  • All top Congress leaders arrested immediately on Aug 9
  • Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted Congress flag at Gowalia Tank, Bombay
  • Underground movement led by Jayaprakash Narayan, Aruna Asaf Ali
  • Usha Mehta ran secret "Voice of Freedom" radio transmitter
  • Parallel govts established: Satara (Maharashtra), Ballia (UP), Midnapur (Bengal)
  • Sucheta Kripalani led non-violent resistance
  • Cripps Mission (1942) had already failed
  • British response: brutal repression — 100,000+ arrested
  • This was truly a people's movement — no central leadership
  • Subhas Chandra Bose simultaneously formed INA (1943)

🏰 Rise of Regional Powers After Mughal Decline

"After Aurangzeb's death (1707), Mughal authority crumbled. Into this vacuum rose powerful regional successor states — Hyderabad, Mysore, Bengal, Awadh, Kerala — which the British cleverly exploited one by one." — From the transcript: Regional States & Their Rise
📅 Hyderabad State

🕌 Hyderabad — Nizam ul-Mulk (Asaf Jah)

  • Founded by Nizam ul-Mulk (Asaf Jah I) in 1724, also known as Kilich Khan
  • Was one of the leading nobles of post-Aurangzeb era
  • Received Deccan Viceroyalty in 1720; defeated all rivals 1720–22
  • Served as Mughal Wazir 1722–24, frustrated by Emperor Muhammad Shah's incapacity
  • Returned to Deccan and founded Hyderabad — never openly declared independence
  • Adopted tolerant policy toward Hindus; appointed Puran Chand (Hindu) as Dewan
  • Implemented Mughal-style jagirdari system with orderly administration
  • Kept powerful Marathas outside his dominion
  • After his death (1748) — Hyderabad began to disintegrate
⚔️ Mysore Kingdom

🐯 Mysore — Hyder Ali & Tipu Sultan

🦁

Hyder Ali (1721–1782)

Commander → King of Mysore
  • Started career as small officer in Mysore army
  • Recognized importance of Western military training — applied it to troops
  • Overthrew King Nanjraj (Wadiya Dynasty) in 1761; established authority
  • Captured: Bednur, Sunda, Sera, Canara, Malabar
  • Adopted policy of religious tolerance
  • Constantly fought Maratha sardars, Nizam, British
  • Died 1782; succeeded by son Tipu Sultan
🐯

Tipu Sultan (1750–1799)

"Tiger of Mysore"
  • Introduced new calendar, coin system, weights & measures
  • Army used muskets & bayonets made in European fashion — manufactured in Mysore itself
  • 1796: Attempted to build a modern navy
  • Sent ambassadors to France seeking alliance against British
  • Used iron-cased rockets in warfare — world's first modern rocket artillery
  • Planted "Tree of Liberty" at Seringapatam, called himself "Citizen Tipu"
  • Died fighting at Seringapatam — 4th Anglo-Mysore War (1799)

⚔️ Four Anglo-Mysore Wars at a Glance

WarYearCauseResultKey Outcome
1st 1767–1769 EIC allied with Nizam & Marathas against Hyder Ali 🤝 Hyder Ali won — Treaty of Madras Mutual defence pact; EIC humiliated
2nd 1780–1784 EIC broke Treaty of Madras; supported Marathas against Hyder Ali ⚖️ Stalemate — Treaty of Mangalore (1784) Status quo restored; Hyder Ali died (1782); Tipu took over

Tipu Sultan's rockets were so advanced that the British captured them and sent them to Woolwich Arsenal in England for study. These inspired William Congreve's Congreve Rockets, later used in Napoleonic Wars. The NASA historian Frank Winter called Tipu's rockets "the most sophisticated in 18th century Asia."
🏛️ Awadh (Oudh)

🌺 Kingdom of Awadh

  • Founded by Saadat Khan Burhan ul-Mulk as an independent principality
  • 1723: Introduced new revenue settlement — equitable land revenue system
  • Protected peasants from exploitation by big zamindars
  • No discrimination between Hindus & Muslims (like Hyderabad)
  • Safdar Jung (successor): Mughal Wazir 1748; Alliance with Maratha sardars; established equitable justice
  • Awadh's tragedy: Nawabs were short-sighted — underestimated EIC threat
  • 1856: Lord Dalhousie annexed Awadh on "misgovernance" pretext — this enraged soldiers (from Awadh!) who triggered 1857 revolt

💥 Awadh's Role in 1857

  • Majority of Bengal Army sepoys were from Awadh
  • Annexation of their homeland in 1856 deeply enraged soldiers
  • Begum Hazrat Mahal led revolt from Lucknow
  • Most intense fighting was in Awadh — "Siege & Relief of Lucknow" lasted months
  • This is why British called it the "Sepoy Mutiny" but historians say "National Uprising"

🌿 Kerala (Travancore Kingdom)

  • In early 18th C, Kerala divided among large number of feudal chiefs
  • King Martanda Varma established Travancore Kingdom after 1729
  • His empire extended from Kanyakumari to Cochin
  • Organized strong army on Western model with modern weapons
  • Built irrigation works, roads, canals for communications
  • Actively encouraged foreign trade
  • Battle of Colachel (1741): Travancore defeated Dutch — only Asian power to defeat Europeans in a pitched battle in 18th century!
  • Dutch commander De Lannoy was captured; later trained Travancore army
  • Successors: Rama Varma (Dharma Raja) further modernized state
  • 1795: Travancore became British subsidiary state
💰 Economic Impact of British RuleNEW

💰 Drain of Wealth — De-Industrialisation — Land Revenue

"The economic bleeding of India was not accidental — it was the deliberate policy of a colonial power. India went from having 27% of global GDP in 1700 to just 3% by 1950." — Dadabhai Naoroji's "Drain of Wealth" Theory (Poverty and Un-British Rule in India, 1901)
27%India's share of global GDP (1700)
3%India's share of global GDP (1950)
₹100 CrAnnual drain estimated by Naoroji
45MWeavers displaced from Indian textile trade
🕳️

Drain of Wealth Theory

  • Dadabhai Naoroji — first to systematically analyze economic drain
  • Drain = excess of exports over imports WITHOUT any return benefit to India
  • Forms: Home Charges (salaries, pensions, debt interest paid to UK), profit remittances, raw material exports at low price, finished goods imports at high price
  • RCDutt, GVJoshi also contributed to this analysis
  • Naoroji called it "Un-British Rule" — unconscious bleeding of India
🏭

De-Industrialisation

  • Indian handicrafts (especially textiles) destroyed by cheap British machine-made goods
  • Dhaka muslin — finest cloth in world — production collapsed; weavers starved
  • Legendary: British cut off thumbs of Dhaka weavers (disputed but symbolic of destruction)
  • Industrial policy: India became raw material supplier + market for British goods
  • No Industrial Revolution in colonial India — deliberately prevented
🌾

Land Revenue Systems

  • Permanent Settlement (1793) — Cornwallis; Bengal, Bihar, Orissa; Zamindars fixed tax forever; peasants had no rights
  • Ryotwari System — Munro; Madras Presidency; direct settlement with ryots (farmers); high assessments caused distress
  • Mahalwari System — village/mahal unit; North India; collective responsibility for revenue
  • All systems resulted in: high taxation, no investment in agriculture, impoverishment of peasants

🧂 Chapter 11c — Dandi MarchNEW

🧂 Civil Disobedience Movement (1930) — Dandi March

"With this, I am shaking the foundations of the British Empire." — Mahatma Gandhi, picking up salt at Dandi beach, April 6, 1930 — From the transcript: Dandi March significance

🎯 Why SALT as the Issue?

  • Salt — the most universal necessity — rich, poor, Hindu, Muslim ALL need it
  • Salt Tax was most unjust — taxing a basic survival necessity
  • Visible, symbolic, easily understood by masses
  • Easy to participate — anyone could make salt on any coast
  • No violence required — just law-breaking
  • Could not be countered without extreme repression (bad PR for British)

📊 Mass Participation in CDM

  • 🚺 Women: Massive participation — first time in this scale; picketing shops
  • 👨‍🎓 Students & Youth: Left schools, joined marches
  • 🏕️ Tribes: Central Provinces, Maharashtra, Karnataka
  • 👷 Workers: Bombay, Calcutta, Madras, Sholapur
  • 🌾 Peasants: UP, Bihar, Gujarat — No Revenue Campaign
  • 🕌 Muslims: Less than NCM (Muslim League advised against)

📜 Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931) — Terms

British Concessions:
  • All non-violent political prisoners released
  • Fines not yet collected — remitted
  • Confiscated land not yet sold — returned
  • Government servants who resigned — lenient treatment
  • Coastal villagers allowed to make salt for domestic use
Congress Concessions:
  • Suspend Civil Disobedience Movement
  • Participate in Round Table Conference (2nd)
  • No demand for inquiry into police excesses
Critics: Gandhi gave up too much. Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev executed March 23, 1931 despite Pact — Gandhi could not save them.

🔥 QUIT INDIA — BACKGROUND

💥 Why 1942?

  • WWII going badly for Britain
  • Japan reached India's borders (Burma fell)
  • Cripps Mission (1942) failed — "Post-dated cheque on crashing bank" (Gandhi)
  • Indians unwilling to cooperate with war effort without freedom

📋 Cripps Mission (1942)

  • Stafford Cripps sent by Churchill
  • Offered Dominion status AFTER war
  • Provinces could opt out (implied Pakistan)
  • Congress rejected: no immediate power transfer
  • Gandhi's comment: "A post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"

🎯 Resolution

  • August 8, 1942 — AICC Bombay
  • Called for immediate end to British rule
  • If British left, independent India would join Allies
  • Gandhi gave "Do or Die" mantra

🚨 British Response

  • Gandhi, entire Working Committee arrested on Aug 9
  • All INC leaders jailed — but movement continued!
  • Underground networks: Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia
  • Usha Mehta's secret radio transmitter

🌊 Spread of the Movement

  • Bombay: Mass strikes, Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted INC flag at Gowalia Tank
  • Satara (Maharashtra): Nana Patil established Parallel Government ("Prati Sarkar")
  • Ballia (UP): Chittu Pandey briefly established parallel government
  • Midnapore (Bengal): Tamluk & Contai — Jatiya Sarkar (Parallel Govt) formed
  • Usha Mehta: Secret "Congress Radio" broadcast from Bombay
  • JP Narayan: Escaped from Hazaribagh jail; led underground movement

📊 Significance of QIM

  • First truly leaderless mass movement — cadres acted independently
  • Last major mass movement before independence
  • Demonstrated India's determination — British began thinking of exit
  • Parallel governments showed Indians could self-govern
  • Hindu-Muslim divisions widened (Muslim League didn't join)
  • British Viceroy Linlithgow called it "most serious since 1857"
📜 British Constitutional ActsNEW

📜 Evolution of Constitutional Governance 1773–1947

⭐ Government of India Act 1935 — Most Important Pre-Independence Act

  • Provincial Autonomy introduced — provinces got full responsible govt
  • All-India Federation proposed (never implemented due to Princely States)
  • Dyarchy abolished in provinces; introduced at Centre
  • Bicameral legislatures in 6 provinces
  • Federal Court established (1937)
  • Reserve Bank of India established under this act
  • Separate electorates extended to more communities
  • Burma separated from India
  • Election of 1937: Congress won majority in 8 of 11 provinces
  • This act became the basis of the Indian Constitution (significant parts adopted)
  • REJECTED by Congress — "machine with strong brakes but no engine"
  • Foundation on which independent India's Constitution was built
