🧭 Act I · The Constitutional Map

Public money needs an outside eye. Independence is the method.

The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) is a constitutional office that audits the accounts of the Union, the States and other bodies as Parliament’s law provides. The CAG is not a minister, not a court and not an investigating or prosecuting agency: the CAG audits, reports facts and findings, and leaves political and legal follow-up to the Legislature, the executive and the courts.

📖V

Part V · Chapter V · Articles 148–151

The CAG provisions sit in Part V (The Union), Chapter V. They cover office and independence (148), duties and powers (149), form of accounts (150) and audit reports (151).

🧮279

Part XII · Article 279

Separately, Article 279 in the finance Part makes the CAG’s certificate final on the “net proceeds” of taxes and duties, for purposes of that Chapter.

🕰️1860

Origins

The office of Auditor-General of India dates from 1860. The constitutional CAG took effect on 26 January 1950.

📍DELHI

Institution

The CAG heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department (IA&AD), headquartered in New Delhi. Office-holder, strength and statistics change — verify on the official site.

🛡️

Why constitutional status matters

The office, its removal protection and the charged nature of its expenses come from the Constitution itself, not an executive order. The detailed audit mandate, however, is statutory (Article 149).

🐕

“Watchdog” is a metaphor

A popular label — not constitutional text. The CAG has no power to punish, to cancel a decision or to stop spending; findings take effect through Legislature, executive action and courts.

🚨 Three distinctions: audit ≠ accounts compilation (since 1976 the Union’s accounts are compiled by the Government, not the CAG) · certifying net proceeds ≠ distributing taxes · reporting to the President ≠ the President directing the audit.

🕰️ Act II · The Historical Corridor

From colonial treasury control to constitutional audit.

The institution grew in stages: first an audit officer within the colonial administration, then growing statutory protection, and finally a constitutional office. Dates below are limited to well-established milestones; confirm finer details in official histories.

1860 · AUDITOR-GENERAL OF INDIA

The office of Auditor-General was created; Sir Edward Drummond is recorded as the first holder.

1919 · GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT

Strengthened the Auditor-General’s independence from the executive: he was appointed by the Secretary of State rather than by the Government of India’s own executive.

1935 · GOVERNMENT OF INDIA ACT

Gave the Auditor-General statutory safeguards in the federal scheme, including removal only in the manner and on the grounds applicable to a Federal Court Judge.

1948 · V. NARAHARI RAO

Appointed Auditor-General; he continued as the first CAG under the Constitution (commonly listed as 1948–54).

26 JANUARY 1950 · THE CONSTITUTION

Articles 148–151 took effect; the CAG became a constitutional office. Article 279 entered the finance framework.

1971 · CAG (DPC) ACT

The Comptroller and Auditor-General’s (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971 gave Article 149 its detailed content, including the six-year term and age-65 rule.

1976 · SEPARATION OF ACCOUNTS FROM AUDIT

For the Union, accounts compilation moved from the CAG to the departments (Departmentalisation of Union Accounts), leaving the CAG with audit. Most States still use the CAG’s offices for accounts.

2002 · GASAB

The Government Accounting Standards Advisory Board was set up to recommend accounting standards (Article 150 context).

16 NOVEMBER · AUDIT DIWAS

Observed each year since 2021 to mark the institution’s history. Motto: Lokahitarth Satyanishtha — “dedicated to truth in public interest”.

🚨 Trap: the six-year term and the age limit of 65 are in the 1971 Act, not in the text of Article 148. Article 148 fixes the removal rule, not the tenure length.

ARTICLE148👑 Office, Oath & IndependenceWarrant · removal · oath · pay · no later office · staff rules · charged expenses
THE CORE RULE

The CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal and can be removed only in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

📜 Article 148 clause map
ClauseRulePrecision point
148(1)Appointment by President’s warrant under hand and seal; removal only like a Supreme Court Judge.Removal needs an address of both Houses of Parliament with the prescribed majorities, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity (Art. 124(4) model).
148(2)Oath or affirmation before the President (or a person he appoints) in the Third Schedule form.The oath is a pledge to uphold the Constitution and act without fear or favour.
148(3)Salary and service conditions as Parliament determines by law; until then as in the Second Schedule. Pay, leave, pension and retirement age cannot be varied to the CAG’s disadvantage after appointment.Pay is set equal to a Supreme Court Judge’s under the 1971 Act and Second Schedule.

🛡️

Tenure security

Removal protection is constitutional. The six-year term and age-65 ceiling come from the 1971 Act.

💰

Financial autonomy

Charged expenses are not voted as a demand for grant, but budgeting, appropriation and audit rules still apply.

🚫

Post-tenure bar

Stricter than for Supreme Court Judges, who are barred from practising before courts but not from every office.

🚨 Trap: do not say “removed by impeachment” as if it were a named procedure in Art. 148 — the Article borrows the Supreme Court Judge standard. And do not say the Constitution fixes a six-year term.

🎯148(1)

Establishment & appointment

The Constitution provides for a CAG appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal. 🎯 A constitutional office, not a statutory body. 👑 Appointment by Presidential warrant. ⚖️ Removal only like a Supreme Court Judge (Art. 124 standard). Tags: Presidential appointment · Warrant under seal · Constitutional office.

📜148(2)

Oath of office

Before the President (or a person he appoints), in the Third Schedule form: faith and allegiance to the Constitution, upholding India’s sovereignty and integrity, performing duties without fear or favour, affection or ill-will. Tags: Constitutional oath · Third Schedule · Solemn commitment.

💰148(3)

Salary & conditions of service

Fixed by Parliament’s law; until then by the Second Schedule. Governed today by the CAG (DPC) Act, 1971, with salary equal to a Supreme Court Judge’s. Parliament, not the executive, holds the ultimate authority. Neither pay nor leave, pension or retirement age may be varied to the CAG’s disadvantage after appointment.

🚫148(4)

Post-retirement restriction

No further office under the Government of India or any State Government. 🚫 A bar on government office. 🎯 Removes the temptation of post-retirement favours. ⚖️ Stricter than for Supreme Court Judges. Whether a particular post (for example, in a public enterprise) counts as “office” needs legal analysis.

🏢148(5)

Administrative powers (staff)

Conditions of service of persons in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department and the CAG’s administrative powers are prescribed by Presidential rules after consulting the CAG. 🏢 Departmental autonomy · 🤝 mandatory consultation · 👥 protection of audit staff from executive pressure.

💰148(6)

Financial independence

The office’s administrative expenses, including staff pay, allowances and pensions, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India: no annual vote on that item, so the executive cannot starve the office of funds. Judicial parity: comparable protection to the courts’ charged expenses.

ARTICLE149🔍 Duties & PowersAs prescribed by Parliament’s law — the 1971 Act
THE OPERATIVE RULE

The CAG performs duties and exercises powers relating to the accounts of the Union, the States and any other authority or body as Parliament prescribes by law; until such law, the pre-1950 Auditor-General’s duties continued.

📚 Core of the CAG (DPC) Act, 1971 — verify section numbers in the current text
AreaWhat the Act provides (summary)Note
Union & State accounts (s. 13)Audit of expenditure from the Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund and Public Account, and of departmental commercial accounts.Legality, authority and regularity of spending are core checks.
Substantially financed bodies (s. 14)Audit of bodies financed by grants or loans from the Consolidated Fund above the statutory threshold (not less than ₹25 lakh and 75% of expenditure); other cases need Presidential or Governor’s sanction.“Substantially financed” is a defined test, not a loose phrase.
Receipts & stores (ss. 16–17)Audit of Union and State receipts and of stores and stock accounts.Revenue audit is part of the mandate.
Access (s. 18)Authority to inspect offices of accounts, require records and call for information.Statutory authority to inspect and ask; read the Act for limits.
Corporations & other bodies (ss. 19–20)Audit as the founding law provides, or by agreement at the request of the President, Governor or Administrator.Government companies are governed by the Companies Act, 2013 (below).

🏢 Government companies

  • A government company is one in which the Central or State Governments (or both) hold not less than 51% of paid-up share capital (Companies Act, 2013, s. 2(45)).
  • The CAG appoints the statutory auditors (chartered accountants) and then conducts a supplementary or test audit (ss. 139 and 143(6)). Many sources wrongly say financial and compliance audits of PSUs are “discretionary” and performance audits “obligatory”: the statutory-auditor route is mandatory; performance audits are selected by the CAG.
🚨 Trap: “Unlimited access and summons power” is an exaggeration. The Act gives the CAG authority to inspect, require documents and call for information; it does not make the CAG a court or an investigating police agency.
🧠 Article 149“Duty by Law” — the Constitution points to Parliament; the 1971 Act supplies the detail.

🔍 The audit universe: types of audit (original study file)

Notes from the original study file, retained and checked against the constitutional and statutory text.
💰1

Financial audit

Verification of financial statements and factual correctness; no misstatement; proper accounting; expenditure within grants; accuracy of revenue collection. Regular, core work.

✅2

Compliance audit

Adherence to rules and regulations: legality, authorisation, Parliamentary directives and procedure; spending as per the Constitution, Acts and rules and budget provisions. Regular, core work.

⚡3

Performance audit

Was the money spent wisely? The 3Es: Economy (lowest cost without sacrificing quality), Efficiency (maximum output from inputs), Effectiveness (intended results). Selected by the CAG.

⚖️4

Propriety audit

Value-for-money lens: wisdom of expenditure, public interest, avoidance of waste and ethical spending. Applied within audits as a standard rather than a separate statutory category.

🏣 Audit of government companies and PSUs

  • Audit of government-controlled and owned public sector undertakings and government companies, ensuring accountability.
  • Government company: not less than 51% of paid-up share capital held by the Central Government, a State Government, or both jointly (Companies Act, 2013, s. 2(45)).
  • PSUs: CPSEs (Central Public Sector Enterprises) and SPSEs (State Public Sector Enterprises).
  • Method: the CAG appoints the statutory auditors (chartered accountants) and then conducts a supplementary or test audit (ss. 139 and 143(6)). Correction: the original file reversed “discretionary” and “obligatory” here; the statutory-auditor route is mandatory, and performance audits are selected by the CAG.

🏛️ Complete audit jurisdiction

⚖️ Legal powers (statutory)

🚪

Access to records

The CAG may require accounts, books, papers and documents relevant to transactions within his duties to be sent for inspection (1971 Act, s. 18). Original wording “unlimited access” is an overstatement; the Act sets the limits.

📞

Questions and information

The CAG may put questions and call for information. The Act does not make the CAG a court with a general summons power.

🔍

Inspection

Authority to inspect offices of accounts, including treasuries and offices keeping initial or subsidiary accounts, and verification of stores and stock.

THE EXACT RULE

CAG reports on Union accounts go to the President, who causes them to be laid before each House of Parliament; reports on a State’s accounts go to the Governor, who causes them to be laid before the State Legislature.

📤 Report types

🚨 Trap: the President or Governor causes reports to be laid; they do not edit them. Union Territory reports follow the relevant UT law — verify for Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir.
🧠 Article 151“P-G-L” — President (Union) · Governor (State) · Laid before the Legislature.

📑 Clause detail (original study file)

👑151(1)

Union audit reports

Reports on the accounts of the Union go to the President, who causes them to be laid before each House of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha). “Shall cause them to be laid” is a duty, not a discretion. Tags: Presidential submission · Parliamentary laying · Constitutional duty.

Reports on a State’s accounts go to the Governor, who causes them to be laid before the State Legislature (Vidhan Sabha, and Vidhan Parishad where one exists). Tags: State Governor · State Legislature · Federal structure.

ARTICLE279🧮 Net Proceeds — Calculation & CertificationCAG certificate final · Parliament’s law or Presidential order for method
THE EXACT RULE

For the purposes of the relevant Chapter, “net proceeds” means proceeds reduced by the cost of collection, ascertained and certified by the CAG, whose certificate is final.

🧮 Article 279 clause map
ClauseRulePrecision point
279(1)“Net proceeds” = proceeds minus cost of collection, for a tax or duty or part of it in or attributable to an area; the CAG ascertains and certifies; the certificate is final.The CAG calculates and certifies. The CAG does not decide the sharing formula.
279(2)A law of Parliament or an order of the President may provide how proceeds are calculated, when and how payments are made, adjustments between years and incidental matters.The method comes from Parliament or the President, not from the CAG.

🔎 Clause detail (original study file)

Notes from the original study file, retained and checked against the constitutional and statutory text.
🧾279(1)

Definition & certification

“Net proceeds” means proceeds reduced by the cost of collection; for any tax or duty, or part, in or attributable to an area, the CAG ascertains and certifies it and the certificate is final. Focus: calculation and certification. Tags: Final certification · Net proceeds calculation.

👑279(2)

Parliament’s law and Presidential order

Subject to clause (1) and other express provisions of the Chapter, a law of Parliament or an order of the President may provide for the manner of calculating proceeds, timing and manner of payments, adjustments between financial years and incidental matters. Focus: Presidential and Parliamentary powers.

⚖️ Role of the CAG

  • 📍 Determines how much tax was collected
  • ➖ Subtracts the cost of collection
  • 📊 Certifies the final “net proceeds” amount
  • ⚖️ The certificate is final within this constitutional scheme
  • 🏦 Technical calculation, not distribution

📜 Tax distribution is governed by

  • Article 270: distribution of taxes between the Union and the States
  • Finance Commission (Article 280): recommends the sharing formula
  • Parliament and the President: give effect through law or order

📜 Quasi-judicial finality, technical ascertainment, federal trust

  • ⚖️ The certificate’s finality means it is not reopened within the constitutional scheme (original: “cannot be challenged in court”; read as finality, not immunity from every legal check).
  • 💰 Technical ascertainment of the divisible pool.
  • 🏛️ Both the Union and the States rely on the CAG’s neutrality.
  • 🔄 Clause (2) permits year-to-year adjustments through law or order.

🔁 Act V · The Accountability Loop

Audit finds. The Legislature questions.

Article 151 sends the report to the Legislature, where committees carry the findings to the executive. The three actors have different jobs.

🔍

CAG — the auditor

Independent constitutional auditor that examines accounts and reports findings. Traditionally called the PAC’s “friend, philosopher and guide”.

🔎22

PAC and COPU

The Public Accounts Committee has 22 members (15 Lok Sabha, 7 Rajya Sabha); the Chair by convention goes to an Opposition member. The Committee on Public Undertakings examines PSU reports.

🏢

Executive — the implementer

Ministries respond, explain and submit action-taken notes on the recommendations accepted.

The CAG’s word is evidence, not a verdict: findings reach the public through the Legislature, and consequences follow only through lawful political, administrative or judicial processes.Suggested synthesis — not attributed to any person or court.
🚨 Trap: the PAC is a parliamentary review committee, not a prosecutor. Do not give a fixed “six-month” action-taken deadline unless you have checked the current procedure.

🔭 Act VIII · Reform Horizons

Strong constitutional design. Open practical questions.

The points below are debates and directions, not settled law. Verify current positions in official records.

📒

Accrual accounting

GASAB has worked on moving government accounts from cash to accrual; adoption is gradual and varies across governments.

💻

Digital and data audit

Audit increasingly uses data analytics; the legal mandate stays that of the 1971 Act and related laws.

Timeliness

Debate continues on how quickly accounts are finalised, reports tabled and action-taken notes completed.

🎯 Scenario cards: decide before revealing

👑 Can the President dismiss the CAG at will?

No. Article 148(1) protects the CAG: removal only like a Supreme Court Judge.

🚫 May a former CAG become a Governor?

No. Article 148(4) bars further office under the Union or any State Government.

📝 Does the CAG decide how net proceeds are shared with States?

No. The CAG certifies net proceeds (Art. 279(1)); sharing follows Article 270, the Finance Commission under Article 280, and law.

📒 Who prescribes the form of State accounts?

The President, on the CAG’s advice (Article 150).

🏢 A company is 40% government-owned. Is it a “government company”?

No. The statutory test is not less than 51% of paid-up capital; other routes (e.g., s. 14 financing tests) may still apply.

📤 A State audit report is ready. Who lays it before the Legislature?

The Governor causes it to be laid (Article 151(2)).

💰 Do charged expenses mean the CAG office needs no budget or audit?

No. Charged status removes the vote on that item; budgeting, appropriation and audit rules continue.

⚡ Rapid recall: the precision round

1. Which Articles contain the CAG provisions?

Articles 148–151 (Part V, Chapter V), plus Article 279 in the finance Part.

2. Who appoints the CAG?

The President, by warrant under hand and seal (148(1)).

3. Where are the six-year term and age 65 fixed?

In the CAG (DPC) Act, 1971, not in Article 148 itself.

4. Which clause bars later government office?

Article 148(4).

5. Which clause charges the office’s expenses on the Consolidated Fund?

Article 148(6); the CAG’s salary is also charged under 112(3)(c).

6. What does Article 149 depend on?

Parliament’s law — today the 1971 Act.

7. What is the test for a government company?

51% or more paid-up share capital held by the Centre, States or both.

8. Who prescribes the form of accounts?

The President on the CAG’s advice (150).

9. To whom do Union and State audit reports go?

President (Union) and Governor (State), who cause them to be laid (151).

10. How many members does the PAC have?

22 (15 Lok Sabha and 7 Rajya Sabha).

11. Who certifies net proceeds?

The CAG, and the certificate is final (279(1)).

12. Who may provide the method of calculating proceeds?

A law of Parliament or an order of the President (279(2)).

13. Since when has the CAG been constitutional?

26 January 1950.

14. What changed in 1976?

Union accounts were separated from audit; the CAG retained audit.

A strong answer connects constitutional design with purpose: status, mandate, safeguards, reporting, then limits and reform. Do not claim the CAG can enforce or prosecute.

📚 A compact constitutional glossary

🏛️ Constitutional office
An office whose essential framework comes from the Constitution; detailed powers may be statutory.
📜 Statutory mandate
Duties set by legislation — here the 1971 Act under Article 149.
💰 Charged expenditure
Expenditure not put to the vote as a demand for grant; budgeting and audit still apply.
📒 Finance Accounts
Annual statements of the government’s receipts, expenditure, assets and liabilities.
📘 Appropriation Accounts
Comparison of spending against grants and charged appropriations.
🧮 Net proceeds
Proceeds of a tax or duty less the cost of collection, as certified by the CAG.
🔍 Propriety
A test of public interest, prudence and wise spending, applied within audit.
📊 Performance audit
Examination of economy, efficiency and effectiveness of programmes.
