📜 ARTICLE 148: CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION

The Bedrock of Independence & Institutional Autonomy

148 Article
Comptroller and Auditor-General of India

The cornerstone article that establishes CAG as a constitutional authority with unparalleled independence from executive influence, creating an impregnable fortress for financial oversight in the world's largest democracy.

148(1)
Establishment & Appointment

"There shall be a Comptroller and Auditor-General of India who shall be appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal."

🎯 Constitutional Office: Not a statutory body but a constitutional authority, giving it supreme status

👑 Presidential Warrant: Highest level of appointment, similar to Supreme Court judges

⚖️ Security of Tenure: Can only be removed like a Supreme Court Judge (Article 124)

Presidential Appointment Warrant under Seal Constitutional Office
148(2)
Oath of Office

"Every person appointed... shall make and subscribe before the President... an oath or affirmation according to the form set out... in the Third Schedule."

📜 Third Schedule Format: Binds CAG to uphold Constitution, sovereignty, and integrity of India

⚡ Psychological Barrier: Creates solemn constitutional commitment against executive pressure

🎯 Fidelity to Duty: Emphasis on faithful discharge "without fear or favor"

Constitutional Oath Third Schedule Solemn Commitment
148(3)
Salary & Conditions of Service (Salary & Rights)

"The salary and other conditions of service... shall be such as may be determined by Parliament by law..."

"Deals with the conditions of service for the CAG personally (Salary, leave, pension)"

💰 CAG Act, 1971: Currently governs salary, set equal to Supreme Court Judge

⚖️ Parliamentary Control: Ultimate authority lies with Parliament, not executive

🛡️ Non-Votable Salary: Cannot be reduced during tenure for political reasons

SALARY PROTECTION MECHANISM

The salary is charged on Consolidated Fund (Art. 148(6)), meaning:

  • ✅ Not subject to annual parliamentary vote
  • ✅ Cannot be withheld or reduced by executive
  • ✅ Automatic payment from Consolidated Fund
  • ✅ Similar protection as judiciary and President
148(4)
Post-Retirement Restrictions

"The Comptroller and Auditor-General shall not be eligible for further office either under the Government of India or under the Government of any State after he has ceased to hold his office."

🚫 Absolute Ban: Cannot join any government service (Centre, State, PSUs)

🎯 Fearless Final Years: Eliminates temptation for post-retirement favors

⚖️ Unique Safeguard: More stringent than even Supreme Court judges

No Post-Retirement Office Absolute Independence Anti-Corruption Measure
148(5)
Administrative Powers (Staff & Administration)

"Subject to the provisions of this Constitution... the conditions of service of persons serving in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department... shall be prescribed by rules made by the President after consultation with the Comptroller and Auditor-General."

"Deals with the conditions of service for the Staff/Employees working under the CAG"

🏢 Departmental Autonomy: CAG controls Indian Audit & Accounts Department

🤝 Mandatory Consultation: President must consult CAG before making rules

👥 Staff Protection: Prevents executive from demoralizing audit staff

148(6)
Financial Independence

"The administrative expenses of the office of the Comptroller and Auditor-General... shall be charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India."

💰 Charged Expenditure: Not subject to parliamentary vote annually

🛡️ Financial Shield: Executive cannot starve CAG of funds

⚖️ Judicial Parity: Same protection as Supreme Court and High Courts

CONSOLIDATED FUND PROTECTION

The "charged expenditure" mechanism ensures:

Automatic Funding
No annual budget battles
Executive Immunity
Cannot use funds as pressure tool
Operational Freedom
Independent recruitment, infrastructure
THE INDEPENDENCE TRINITY

Article 148 creates a triple-layered independence shield unmatched in constitutional design:

1. TENURE SECURITY

Removal only via impeachment like Supreme Court Judge • 6-year fixed term • Age 65 protection

2. FINANCIAL AUTONOMY

Charged expenditure • Non-votable salary • Control over departmental budget

3. POST-RETIREMENT IMMUNITY

No government jobs • No post-audit favors • Fearless final years in office

📋 ARTICLE 149: AUDIT MANDATE & POWERS

From Financial Compliance to Performance Evaluation

149 Article
Duties and Powers of Comptroller and Auditor-General

Defines the expansive jurisdiction and authority of CAG as the Supreme Audit Institution of India, evolving from traditional financial audit to comprehensive performance evaluation of government programs and schemes.

🔍 THE FOUR-DIMENSIONAL AUDIT UNIVERSE

1. FINANCIAL AUDIT 💰

Traditional compliance checking:

  • Verification of financial statements
  • Factual correctness
  • No misstatement
  • Proper accounting
  • Expenditure within grants
  • Revenue collection accuracy

  • Obligatory: Conducted regularly & mandatory

2. COMPLIANCE AUDIT ✅

Rules & regulations adherence:

  • Legality of expenditure
  • Authorization
  • Parliamentary directives
  • Procedural compliance
  • Expenditure as per law & rules:

    ~ Constitution

    ~ Acts & Rules

    ~ Budget provisions


  • Obligatory: Conducted regularly & mandatory

3. PERFORMANCE AUDIT ⚡

Was the money spent wisely,
The 3Es framework (CAG Act, 1971):

  • Economy: Purchasing resources at the lowest possible cost without compromising quality
  • Efficiency: Getting maximum output from the resources used
  • Effectiveness: Achieving the intended goals & desired results

  • Discretionary: Conducted at the discretion of the CAG

4. PROPRIETY AUDIT ⚖️

Value-for-money principles:

  • Wisdom of expenditure
  • Moral correctness
  • Public interest
  • Ethical spending
  • Avoidance of waste

  • Discretionary: Conducted at the discretion of the CAG

✪ AUDIT OF GOVERNMENT COMPANIES & PSUs 🏣

Audit of govt controlled/owned:

  • Public Sector Undertakings
  • Govt companies

  • CAG audits govt companies to ensure accountability
  • CAG audits (Directly or via Chartered Accountants)

  • Discretionary: Financial & Compliance audits
  • Obligatory: Performance & Propriety audits

  • Government Company: ≥ 51% paid-up share capital is held by:
    ~ Central Government
    ~ State Government
    ~ Or jointly by both

  • PSUs:
    ~ CPSEs - Central Public Sector Enterprises
    ~ SPSEs - State Public Sector Enterprises

📒 ARTICLE 150: ACCOUNTING ARCHITECTURE

Standardizing India's Financial Blueprint

150 Article
Form of Accounts of the Union and of the States

Creates a constitutional partnership between the Executive (President) and the Auditor (CAG) to prescribe uniform accounting standards across all governments in India, ensuring transparency, comparability, and audit efficiency.

Essence
Article 150

"The accounts of the Union and the States shall be maintained in a format prescribed by the President of India
‎ ‎ ‎President acts on the advice of the CAG"
"Union & State Govts Maintain accounts in that prescribed format"

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ 🎯 Mandatory Consultation: President must act "on the advice of CAG"

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ 🏛️ Federal Uniformity: Same format for Union and all State accounts

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ 🔧 Technical Primacy: CAG's accounting expertise guides executive prescription

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ 🗒️ Form of Accounts : The structure and system of government accounting :-

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎• Classification of receipts & expenditure

‎ ‎ ‎‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎• Standard financial statements format

HISTORICAL CONTEXT & CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY DEBATES

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar during Constituent Assembly debates:
"Uniform accounts are not merely an accounting requirement; they are the foundation of accountable governance in a federal polity. Without uniform accounts, we cannot have comparable data, we cannot have fair distribution of resources, and we cannot have effective audit."

The concept originated in Government of India Act, 1919 and was strengthened in Government of India Act, 1935. The framers recognized that without uniform accounts, India's complex federal finance system would be unmanageable.

📑 ARTICLE 151: ACCOUNTABILITY BRIDGE

Transforming Audit Findings into Democratic Accountability

151 Article
Audit Reports

Establishes the constitutional pathway for audit reports to reach legislatures, creating the essential bridge between technical audit findings and political accountability through parliamentary scrutiny.

151(1)
Union Audit Reports

"The reports of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India relating to the accounts of the Union shall be submitted to the President, who shall cause them to be laid before each House of Parliament."

👑 Presidential Conduit: Reports go through President as constitutional head

⚖️ Mandatory Laying: "Shall cause them to be laid" imposes duty, not discretion

🏛️ Parliamentary Access: Both Houses (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha) receive reports

Presidential Submission Parliamentary Laying Constitutional Duty
151(2)
State Audit Reports

"The reports of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India relating to the accounts of a State shall be submitted to the Governor of the State, who shall cause them to be laid before the Legislature of the State."

🏛️ Federal Parallel: Similar process at state level

👑 Gubernatorial Role: Governor acts as constitutional conduit for states

⚖️ State Legislature: Reports go to Vidhan Sabha (and Vidhan Parishad where exists)

State Governor State Legislature Federal Structure

279 Article
Calculation of "Net Proceeds", etc.

Makes CAG the final certifying authority for net proceeds of taxes shared between Centre and States, positioning it as a trusted neutral arbiter in India's complex fiscal federalism with quasi-judicial finality.

279(1)
Definition & Certification Authority of Net Proceeds

"'Net proceeds' means, in relation to any tax or duty, the proceeds thereof reduced by the cost of collection... shall be ascertained and certified by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, whose certificate shall be final."

‎


"Clause (1) empowers the CAG to calculate and certify what constitutes net proceeds of a tax."
"This is for taxes that have to be distributed between Centre & States."
🔎Focus = "Calculation & Certification"

‎

💰 Technical Ascertainment: Complex calculation of divisible tax pool

🏛️ Federal Trust: Both Centre and States accept CAG's neutrality

‎

Role of CAG : "CAG only calculates and certifies the net proceeds of a tax"

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎~ Determines how much tax was collected 💰
‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎~ Subtracts cost of collection ➖
‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎~ Certifies the final “net proceeds” amount 📊

🏦 CAG’s role = "Technical calculation, NOT distribution"

📜 HISTORICAL CONTINUITY & EVOLUTION

Historical Roots: The concept of CAG certifying net proceeds dates to Government of India Act, 1935, which first established federal finance arrangements in British India. The framers of the Constitution retained this mechanism recognizing its effectiveness.

Post-Independence Evolution: With the establishment of Finance Commission under Article 280, Article 279's role became even more critical. The Finance Commission uses CAG-certified figures to determine state shares, creating a seamless constitutional mechanism for federal finance.

GST Era Significance: After GST implementation (2017), Article 279's importance multiplied as GST represents the largest divisible pool (approximately 50% of total tax revenue). CAG's certification now affects over ₹18 lakh crore annually.
