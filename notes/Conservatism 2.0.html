📖 1. Introduction to Conservatism

🔤 Term Origin

The word "Conservatism" was first used by François-René de Chateaubriand in 1818 during the Bourbon Restoration in France. He launched a journal titled Le Conservateur — to intellectually counter the lingering momentum and radical changes brought by the French Revolution (1789).

⚙️ Core Definition

Conservatism is a political ideology that seeks to conserve established institutions, traditions, customs, and political systems. It is deeply skeptical of rapid, abstract, or untested changes — preferring slow, organic, prudent evolution to revolutionary upheaval.

To be conservative is to prefer the familiar to the unknown, to prefer the tried to the untried, fact to mystery, the actual to the possible, the limited to the unbounded, the near to the distant, the sufficient to the superabundant, the convenient to the perfect, present laughter to utopian bliss. — Michael Oakeshott, On Being Conservative (1956)

🏛️ The Three Core Beliefs

  1. 📜 Support for Tradition — the accumulated wisdom of generations is wiser than any single mind.
  2. 👤 Belief in Human Imperfection — humanity is flawed; utopian designs are dangerous.
  3. 🌳 Upholding the Organic Structure of Society — society is a living body, not a machine to be redesigned.
💡 Key Insight: Conservatism is less a doctrine and more a disposition — a sensibility that respects the inherited, distrusts the speculative, and venerates the bonds of community across generations.

📜 2. Origin of Conservatism

🌍 Historical Context

Conservatism developed in 19th century Europe as a direct reaction to two great modern ideologies: Liberalism (which championed individual rights and rationalism) and Socialism (which sought to overturn social hierarchy). It was a defensive philosophy — the voice of throne, altar, and ancestral hearth.

⚔️ The Core Challenge — Rejecting the Revolutionary Trinity

Conservatives rejected the French Revolution's celebrated trinity:

❌ Liberty (as abstract right) ❌ Equality (as flattened sameness) ❌ Fraternity (as forced universalism)

In its place, they defended an authoritative, traditional, and hierarchical socio-political order — what Joseph de Maistre summarized as "Throne and Altar."

👤 Edmund Burke — The Father of Modern Conservatism

Edmund Burke (1729–1797), the Irish-born British statesman and philosopher, provided the foundational modern conservative perspective in his masterwork Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790).

🔄 Burke's central insight: Conservatism is NOT blind resistance to change. Rather, it is a prudent willingness to "change in order to conserve." Just as a living organism must adapt to survive, so must institutions — but adaptation must respect the wisdom of inheritance, not the arrogance of abstraction.

‎ "A state without the means of some change is without the means of its conservation."
[ For a government or society to survive, it must be flexible enough to make necessary reforms. If a state refuses to adapt or changes with the times, it will eventually collapse or be destroyed by revolution, failing to "conserve" (preserve) itself ]
In short: adapt to survive — Edmund Burke, Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790)

🔑 3. Central Themes & Core Principles

1️⃣ 🕰️ Tradition — The Backbone of Conservatism

  • 🧵 Central Theme: The defense of tradition is the very backbone of conservative thought.
  • 🙏 Religious Faith: The emphasis on tradition often reflects a belief in a divine creator. Traditional customs are seen as "God-given" — sacred inheritances, not human inventions.
  • 🧠 Accumulated Wisdom: Traditions represent the tested wisdom of past generations. Institutions that have survived for centuries did so because they work — there is a Darwinian logic to inheritance.
  • ⏳ Tested by Time: What has been preserved across generations is fit to be preserved for the living and the unborn.
  • 🆔 Identity & Belonging: Tradition provides individuals with identity, rootedness, and a sense of place — protection against the modern disease of alienation.
Society is indeed a contract... but the state ought not to be considered as nothing better than a partnership agreement in a trade of pepper and coffee... It is a partnership in all science; a partnership in all art; a partnership in every virtue, and in all perfection. As the ends of such a partnership cannot be obtained in many generations, it becomes a partnership not only between those who are living, but between those who are living, those who are dead, and those who are to be born. — Edmund Burke (Burke's Partnership Metaphor)
🌳 The Burkean Trust: We are not absolute owners of society — we are trustees. We inherit it from the dead, hold it for the living, and bequeath it to the unborn. To rashly redesign it is to break faith with the dead and rob the unborn.

2️⃣ 👤 Human Imperfection — The Hobbesian Foundation

  • 📚 O'Sullivan's Famous Phrase: Noel O'Sullivan called conservatism the "philosophy of human imperfection."
  • 📖 Hobbesian Foundation: Human nature is flawed, fearful, rational-but-limited. We are not angels; we are creatures of passion, self-interest, and shortsightedness.
  • 🧐 Mistrust of Abstract Rationality: Conservatives place little trust in abstract reason to design perfect societies. The Enlightenment's faith in pure logic to engineer utopia is, to them, dangerous arrogance.
  • 🏗️ Consequence: Because humans are imperfect, we must rely on time-tested institutions, customs, and authority — not on rational blueprints for reform or revolution.
  • ⛪ Theological Echo: This connects to the Christian doctrine of original sin — humanity is fallen, and any politics that ignores this is doomed to disappointment or tyranny.
What is the use of discussing a man's abstract right to food or to medicine? The question is upon the method of procuring and administering them. In that deliberation I shall always advise to call in the aid of the farmer and the physician, rather than the professor of metaphysics. — Edmund Burke

3️⃣ 🌳 Organic Society — Doing One's Duty

  • 🤝 Security-Seeking Beings: Humans are dependent creatures who cannot exist meaningfully outside society. We are born into webs of relationship, not contracted into them.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Anti-Individualism: Liberal individualism is, for conservatives, a myth. A person is shaped by — and inseparable from — the social groups that formed them: family, friends, colleagues, community, church, nation.
  • ⚖️ Rights vs. Duties: A society where people know only their rights (but not their duties) is rootless, atomistic, and ultimately self-destructive.
  • 🔗 The True Glue of Society: Duty, obligation, loyalty, gratitude — not contracts or rights — hold society together and make true liberty possible.
  • 🌿 The Organic Metaphor: Society is like a living tree, not a Lego set. You cannot disassemble it and rebuild it without killing it. Its institutions evolved together over centuries; tearing one out wounds the whole.

4️⃣ ⚖️ Hierarchy & Authority — The Natural Order

  • 📊 Natural Hierarchy: Society is naturally unequal and hierarchical. For conservatives, social equality is both undesirable (it flattens excellence) and unachievable (it contradicts human nature).
  • 🏔️ Unequal Distribution: Power, status, talent, and property are — and will always be — unevenly distributed. The wise society channels inequality into responsibility, not resentment.
  • 🔗 Burke on Liberty & Order:
    The only liberty I mean is a liberty connected with order; that not only exists along with order and virtue, but which cannot exist at all without them. — Edmund Burke
  • 👑 Authority Develops Organically: Legitimate authority is not created by social contracts (as liberals say) — it grows naturally over generations, like the patina on old bronze. Conservatism supports strong, paternalistic authority.
  • 👪 Paternalism Defined: Exercising power over others with the intention of conferring benefit or preventing harm — like a wise father over his children, or a shepherd over his flock. Noblesse oblige: nobility imposes obligation.
⚜️ Aristocratic Insight: "With great power comes great responsibility" is not a Marvel slogan — it is the conservative creed. Hierarchy is justified only when those at the top bear genuine duties toward those below.

📚 4. Types of Conservatism

Conservatism is not monolithic. It splits into distinct streams, each responding to different historical moments and threats.

01

👑 Authoritarian Conservatism

  • 📏 Definition: Practice of government "from above" — authority exercised over the population with or without consent.
  • 🎯 Goal: Restore absolute power to hereditary monarchy and traditional clerical authority.
  • 🇷🇺 Classic Example: Tsar Nicholas I of Russia propagated the doctrine of "Orthodoxy, Autocracy, Nationality" as a direct counter to the revolutionary "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity."
  • 👤 Key Thinkers: Joseph de Maistre, António de Oliveira Salazar (Portugal), Engelbert Dollfuss (Austria).
  • ⚠️ Modern Critique: Often shades into reactionary politics; can be the precursor to fascism if hierarchy is racialized.
02

🏛️ Traditional (Classical) Conservatism

  • 📖 Definition: Emphasizes transcendent moral principles, customs, conventions, and lived traditions.
  • 🌍 Focus: Defends classical Western civilization — its philosophy, religion, art, and political wisdom.
  • 📏 Values: Committed to hierarchical and paternalistic values; respect for inherited institutions.
  • 👤 Key Proponent: Michael Oakeshott — perhaps the most important 20th-century conservative philosopher.
  • ✨ Mood: Less about politics, more about a disposition — gratitude, reverence, a love of the familiar.
03

👨‍👧‍👦 Paternalistic Conservatism

  • 👨‍👦 Meaning: From "Pater" (Latin for father) — to act in a fatherly fashion toward society.
  • ⚙️ Political Principle: Exercising power/authority over others to confer benefit or prevent harm.
  • 🇬🇧 British Examples: "One-Nation" conservatism of Benjamin Disraeli; social welfare policies; laws like compulsory seatbelt wearing, anti-smoking laws.
  • 🔄 Note: This type accepts a qualified form of social democracy (post-1945) — the state can intervene to maintain social cohesion.
  • 🎩 Famous Doctrine: Disraeli's "One Nation" — the rich have a duty to the poor; otherwise society fractures into "Two Nations."
04

💼 Liberal Conservatism & The New Right

  • 💡 Core Belief: Economic individualism — "get government off the back of business."
  • ⚠️ Cautious on Social Life: Less willing to extend liberty into social/moral affairs (pessimistic view of human nature persists).
  • 💪 Strong State Required: To maintain public order and ensure authority is respected. "Free economy, strong state."
  • 📈 Free Markets: Attracted to free-market theory because it promises social order through self-regulation and reward for virtue.
  • 🤝 The New Right — A Marriage of Two Traditions:
    1. Classical Liberal Economics (Adam Smith's free market) → critique of "big government"
    2. Traditional Conservatism → defense of order, authority, discipline, family values
  • 🇬🇧🇺🇸 Living Examples: Thatcherism (UK) and Reaganism (USA) — the dominant force from 1979 onward.
05

🇺🇸 Neo-Conservatism

  • 📅 Origin: Emerged in the 1970s in the USA — many founders were ex-liberals or ex-Trotskyists "mugged by reality."
  • 🧬 Definition: A fusion of political individualism + traditional conservatism.
  • 📉 Crisis Diagnosis: The current crisis of society stems from the declining influence of culture, religion, and morality — not economics.
  • ☭ Historical Context: Originated as a counter to Communism, Stalinism, and 1960s counterculture.
  • 🌍 Foreign Policy: Activist, often interventionist — promoting democracy and Western values abroad (e.g., Iraq War 2003).
  • 👤 Key Neo-Conservatives: Irving Kristol (the "Godfather"), Francis Fukuyama, Nathan Glazer, Norman Podhoretz, Leo Strauss (intellectual godfather).

🧠 5. Important Thinkers — A Deep Dive

⚔️ Joseph de Maistre (1753–1821)
The Prophet of Throne and Altar — Extreme / Latin Conservatism
  • 📍 Type: Extreme reactionary; "Latin Conservatism" — Catholic, monarchist, anti-Enlightenment.
  • 🎯 Goal: Restore monarchy and the authority of the Church over the state.
  • 📢 Famous Slogan: Opposed "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity" → proclaimed "Throne and Altar" (Le Trône et l'Autel).
  • 🔥 Key Idea: The executioner is the foundation of society; political authority rests on the willingness to punish. Famously dark vision of human nature.
  • 📖 Famous Works: Considerations on France (1796), St. Petersburg Dialogues (1821).
  • 💭 Legacy: Inspired later authoritarian thinkers across Europe; Isaiah Berlin called him a precursor of 20th-century fascism, though that label is debated.
🏛️ Edmund Burke (1729–1797)
Father of Anglo-American Conservatism & Modern Conservatism
  • 🏆 Titles: Father of Anglo-American Conservatism; founder of Modern Conservatism.
  • 🇮🇪 Background: Irish-born British MP; surprisingly supported the American Revolution (which he saw as defending ancient English liberties) but vehemently opposed the French Revolution (which he saw as destroying everything).
  • 🔄 Key Idea: Pragmatic willingness to "change in order to conserve." Reform, yes — but with reverence, not arrogance.
  • ❌ Opposed: The French revolutionary doctrine of "universal rights of man" — for Burke, wisdom resides in experience, tradition, and history, not abstract reason.
  • 🌳 The "Little Platoons": Burke loved the small communities (family, neighborhood, guild) that bind individuals to society. "To love the little platoon we belong to in society is the first principle of public affections."
  • 📚 Important Books:
    • A Vindication of Natural Society (1756)
    • A Philosophical Enquiry into the Origin of Our Ideas of the Sublime and Beautiful (1757)
    • Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790) — the bible of modern conservatism
📜 Michael Oakeshott (1901–1990)
The Philosopher of Disposition
  • 🇬🇧 Background: British philosopher; professor at the London School of Economics.
  • 🚫 Rejected: Rationalism in politics — the dangerous belief that society can be redesigned by reason from scratch.
  • ✅ Argued For: Traditional values, established customs, and a conservative disposition — a temperament, not a program.
  • 📜 Famous Distinction:
    • Civil Association (societas) — citizens bound by shared rules, free to pursue their own ends. ✅ Good.
    • Enterprise Association (universitas) — society pursued as a single collective project. ❌ Dangerous.
  • 🍳 The "Cookbook" Critique: Rationalists treat politics like a cookbook — follow the recipe and you'll bake the perfect society. But politics is more like cuisine — taste, judgment, tradition, and feel matter more than rules.
  • 📚 Important Books:
    • Rationalism in Politics and Other Essays (1962)
    • On Human Conduct (1975)
    • On History (1983)
💰 Friedrich von Hayek (1899–1992)
The Apostle of Spontaneous Order
  • 🇦🇹 Background: Austrian-British economist and philosopher; Nobel Prize 1974.
  • 📌 Belief: Firm believer in individualism and the market order.
  • ❌ Implacable Critic of: Socialism, central planning, and the welfare state in its expansive forms.
  • 🏛️ "Hayekian Liberalism": A social system that supports and protects the "Great Society" — an open, abstract, market-based, rule-governed order.
  • ⚠️ Important Note: Hayek actually rejected being called a "conservative" (see his essay "Why I Am Not a Conservative") — but his ideas became foundational to the New Right and modern conservatism.
  • 💬 Key Quote:
    Since the development during the last decades has been generally in a socialist direction, it may seem that both conservatives and liberals have been mainly intent on retarding that movement. — F.A. Hayek
  • 🌐 Concept of Spontaneous Order: Complex social institutions (language, market, common law) emerge from countless individual actions — not from any single designer's mind. Trying to "plan" them destroys them.
  • 📚 Important Books:
    • The Road to Serfdom (1944) — most famous; argued central planning leads to tyranny
    • The Constitution of Liberty (1960)
    • Law, Legislation and Liberty (3 vols, 1973–1979)
    • The Fatal Conceit: The Errors of Socialism (1988)
👑 Irving Kristol (1920–2009)
Godfather of Neo-Conservatism
  • 🇺🇸 Background: American journalist and intellectual; co-founded The Public Interest and The National Interest.
  • 🔄 Journey: Started as a young Trotskyist → became a liberal → abandoned liberalism in the 1970s. Famously defined a neoconservative as "a liberal who has been mugged by reality."
  • ❌ Criticized: The spread of welfarism, the 1960s counterculture, moral relativism, and the decline of bourgeois virtues.
  • 🏆 Title: "Godfather of Neo-Conservatism" — shaped a generation of American conservative thought.
  • 💼 "Two Cheers for Capitalism": Capitalism deserves only two cheers, not three — it is the best system, but it cannot sustain itself without the moral capital of religion, family, and tradition that it tends to erode.
  • 📚 Important Books:
    • Two Cheers for Capitalism (1978)
    • Reflections of a Neo-Conservative (1983)
    • Neoconservatism: The Autobiography of an Idea (1995)

🌍 7. Conservatism in Practice — Global Snapshots

🇬🇧 British Conservatism

The Conservative Party (Tories) — one of the oldest political parties in the world. Tradition of Disraelian One-Nation conservatism, transformed by Margaret Thatcher (PM 1979–1990) into free-market New Right conservatism. Today balances tradition (monarchy, Brexit, Church of England) with markets.

🇺🇸 American Conservatism

A unique fusion: economic libertarianism + religious traditionalism + assertive nationalism. Modern movement built by William F. Buckley Jr. (National Review), Russell Kirk, and politically realized by Ronald Reagan (1981–89). Today torn between traditional, neoconservative, libertarian, and populist (MAGA) strands.

🇮🇳 Indian Conservatism

India's conservative tradition has multiple strands: cultural conservatism defending Hindu civilizational heritage; Gandhian conservatism emphasizing village community, swadeshi, and moral order; and modern political conservatism associated with the BJP. Thinkers include Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Sri Aurobindo, and contemporary voices.

🌐 Continental European Conservatism

Christian Democratic parties (Germany's CDU, Italy's former DC) blended Catholic social teaching with market economics and welfare. More state-friendly than Anglo-American conservatism. Currently challenged by both progressive liberalism and right-wing populism.

🧨 8. Critiques & Contemporary Debates

🗽 Liberal Critique

  • Conservatism's defense of "tradition" can preserve unjust hierarchies — slavery, caste, patriarchy were all once "tradition."
  • Skepticism of reason becomes obscurantism; tradition can be a mask for entrenched power.
  • Limits individual autonomy and choice.

☭ Socialist / Marxist Critique

  • Conservatism is the ideology of the ruling class — defending inequalities that benefit elites.
  • "Organic society" rhetoric obscures real class conflict.
  • Paternalism is a velvet glove over an iron fist of exploitation.

♀️ Feminist Critique

  • The "traditional family" defended by conservatives often imprisons women in patriarchal roles.
  • "Natural hierarchy" historically meant male dominance.
  • Conservative resistance to feminist gains (suffrage, reproductive rights, equal pay) is part of conservatism's record.

🌱 Ecological Critique

  • Modern free-market conservatism (New Right) has often resisted environmental regulation.
  • However, some thinkers (Roger Scruton's Green Philosophy) argue conservatism is naturally green — love of homeland, stewardship of inheritance, suspicion of unchecked progress.

🔄 Internal Debates Within Conservatism

  • ⚔️ Tradition vs Market: Free markets often destroy the traditions conservatives love (small towns, family farms, churches). Can you have both?
  • 🌍 National vs Global: Should conservatives defend national sovereignty (Brexit, MAGA) or embrace globalist trade and alliances?
  • 🗽 Liberty vs Authority: How strong should the state be? New Right says small in economy, strong in morals — but is that coherent?
  • 🎭 Elite vs Populist: Is conservatism the philosophy of cultured elites (Burke, Oakeshott) or of "ordinary people" against cosmopolitan elites (modern populism)?
