🎬 Act I · The Constitutional Map

Part XVIII — Three Doors into the Storm

Part XVIII (Articles 352–360) lets the Constitution itself respond to extreme situations. The three principal mechanisms solve different problems: Article 352 answers a security threat, Article 356 answers a breakdown of constitutional government in a State, and Article 360 answers a threat to financial stability or credit. They are exceptional powers — but never constitution-free zones.

352

🚨 National Emergency

War, external aggression or armed rebellion threatens the security of India or any part of it.

Read with Arts. 353, 354, 358, 359, 250, 83(2), 172(1).

356

🏛️ President’s Rule

The government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

Read with Arts. 355, 357, 365. A Governor’s report is possible, not indispensable.

360

💠 Financial Emergency

The financial stability or credit of India or any part of its territory is threatened.

Enables financial-propriety directions, salary reductions and reservation of certain State Bills.

🌍 Origins & philosophy

📚 Borrowed designs

Textbooks trace the national-emergency idea to Art. 48 of the Weimar Constitution and the State-level breakdown idea to Section 93 of the Government of India Act, 1935. These are influences, not substitutes for the present Indian text.

🏛️ Ambedkar’s hope

Introducing the draft provisions in the Constituent Assembly (1949), Dr. B. R. Ambedkar hoped such powers would remain a “dead letter”, and that, if ever used, the President would exercise proper care. Federal India is meant to flex toward the Union in crisis and flex back afterwards.

ARTICLE352🚨 Proclamation of National EmergencySecurity · written Cabinet decision · parliamentary control

If the President is satisfied that a grave emergency exists whereby the security of India or any part of its territory is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion, a Proclamation may be made for the whole of India or a specified part.

📍 Whole or part of India📝 Written Union Cabinet decision🏛️ Approval: 1 month🗳️ Special majority⏳ Renewal: 6 months

📜 Complete clause map — 352(1) to (9)

ClauseRulePrecision point
352(1)Grounds, satisfaction and territorial scope.Explanation: a Proclamation may be made before actual occurrence if the President is satisfied that danger is imminent.
352(2)A Proclamation may be varied or revoked by a later Proclamation.Revocation needs no parliamentary approval.
352(3)The President cannot issue (or vary) a Proclamation unless the Union Cabinet’s decision is communicated in writing.“Union Cabinet” = PM and Cabinet-rank Ministers under Art. 75. Informal advice of the PM alone is insufficient.
352(4)Laid before both Houses; ceases after one month unless both approve by resolution.Proviso (Lok Sabha dissolved): if Rajya Sabha has approved, the Proclamation survives until 30 days after the Lok Sabha first sits, unless it approves within that time.
352(5)An approved Proclamation lasts six months from the date of the second approving resolution; further six-month continuations by resolution of both Houses.Dissolution proviso again protects continuity if Rajya Sabha has approved continuance.
352(6)Special majority for resolutions under (4) and (5).Majority of the total membership AND at least two-thirds of members present and voting — in each House.
352(7)The President must revoke if the Lok Sabha passes a resolution disapproving the Proclamation or its continuance.The (6) special majority does not apply to this disapproval resolution.
352(8)Notice signed by one-tenth of Lok Sabha’s total membership of intent to move disapproval → special sitting within 14 days.Notice goes to the Speaker if the House is in session, otherwise to the President.
352(9)The President may issue different Proclamations on different grounds even if one is already in operation.Concurrent emergencies are constitutionally possible.

⚔️ The three grounds

⚔️ War

A grave security threat arising from war. The Constitution gives no detailed definition, so avoid inventing a rigid “formal declaration” test.

🌍 External aggression

A security threat from outside India. Imminent danger suffices — hostilities need not already have occurred.

🔥 Armed rebellion

Substituted by the 44th Amendment for the vaguer “internal disturbance”. Textbooks call war/aggression emergencies “external” and armed-rebellion ones “internal”.

There is no fixed overall maximum under Art. 352 so long as approvals continue. Both Houses act separately: Rajya Sabha is a co-equal partner in approval and renewal, while only the Lok Sabha has the express disapproval safeguard.

📖 Why a written Cabinet decision? In June 1975 the Proclamation was issued on the Prime Minister’s advice and the Cabinet was informed afterwards. The 44th Amendment turned that experience into a constitutional requirement.
🚨 “One-tenth” is a notice threshold, not the vote that ends an Emergency. It forces a special sitting; disapproval is then decided by the Lok Sabha’s ordinary voting rule, not the approval special majority.

🧭 What a National Emergency does not automatically do

  • It does not dismiss State governments or dissolve State legislatures.
  • It does not suspend every Fundamental Right — Arts. 358 and 359 are separate, conditional mechanisms.
  • It does not automatically extend legislative terms — Parliament must legislate under Arts. 83(2)/172(1).
  • It does not remove judicial review of legality.

ARTICLE353📡 Effect of Proclamation of EmergencyUnion directions · conferral of Union powers · territorial spillover

While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, the federal balance tilts towards the Union — without replacing the State’s institutions.

ProvisionExact functionInsight
353(a)Notwithstanding anything in the Constitution, the Union’s executive power extends to giving directions to any State on the manner of exercising its executive power.Wider than ordinary directions under Arts. 256–257.
353(b)Parliament’s power to legislate on any matter includes power to make laws conferring powers and imposing duties on the Union or its officers and authorities, even if the matter is not in the Union List.More specific than “Parliament gets State List power”. The direct State-List power is Art. 250.
ProvisoIf the Proclamation operates in only a part of India, (a) and (b) can extend to another State if security is threatened by activities in or in relation to that part.Territorial spillover is conditional.

🖋️ Companions: Arts. 250, 251, 83(2), 172(1)

250 · State List

While an Emergency operates, Parliament may legislate for all or part of India on State List matters (the 101st Amendment also links Art. 250 with GST under Art. 246A). Such law, to the extent of the incompetence, ceases six months after the Emergency ends; things already done are saved.

251 · Conflict

If a State law is inconsistent with a Union law made under Art. 250, the State law is inoperative to the extent of repugnancy while the Union law continues. State legislatures are not abolished.

83(2) & 172(1) · Terms

Parliament may by law extend the Lok Sabha / a State Assembly for not more than one year at a time, and never beyond six months after the Emergency ceases.

🧩 Three-part split: 353(a) = Union directions; 353(b) = conferral of Union powers/duties; 250 = Parliament’s State List competence.
🧠 Effects without erasing States353 directs · 250 legislates · 251 resolves conflict · 83/172 stretch terms.
ARTICLE354💰 Revenue Distribution During EmergencyArts. 268–279 · presidential order · financial-year limit

While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation the President may, by order, direct that all or any of the provisions of Articles 268 to 279 (taxes shared between Union and States) have effect subject to specified exceptions or modifications.

ClauseRulePrecision
354(1)Exceptions or modifications for a stated period.The period cannot extend beyond the end of the financial year in which the Proclamation ceases to operate.
354(2)Every order must be laid before each House as soon as may be.“Laid” ≠ a separate approval resolution.

Context outside the express range: the Finance Commission (Art. 280) and its recommendations (Art. 281) are important fiscal background, but they lie outside “268–279”.

🚨 Do not merge 354 and 360. 354 operates during a National Emergency and touches revenue sharing; 360 needs its own Financial Emergency Proclamation and authorises propriety directions and salary/Bill measures.

ARTICLE355🤝 The Union’s Duty Towards StatesProtection · constitutional government · Art. 365 inference
“It shall be the duty of the Union to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance and to ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the provisions of this Constitution.”Article 355
  • Protective duty: against external aggression and internal disturbance.
  • Constitutional duty: ensure State government runs according to the Constitution.

Art. 355 is a duty, not a proclamation and not a free-standing licence for any coercive step; every measure still needs its own constitutional or statutory authority.

📌 “Internal disturbance” still lives in Art. 355. The 44th Amendment replaced it with “armed rebellion” only in Art. 352. A disturbance can call for assistance or lawful directions without meeting the Art. 352 or 356 threshold.

📨 Article 365 — the associated inference (Part XIX)

Where a State fails to comply with or give effect to directions given in exercise of the Union’s executive power under the Constitution, it is lawful for the President to hold that the State government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution. It is not an automatic command to impose President’s Rule; the direction must itself be valid and any Art. 356 proclamation remains reviewable.

ARTICLE356🏛️ President’s Rule in a StateConstitutional breakdown · two-month approval · federal safeguards

If the President, “on receipt of a report from the Governor of a State or otherwise”, is satisfied that the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution, the President may issue a Proclamation.

📨 Governor’s report: not mandatory🏛️ Approval: 2 months🗳️ Ordinary majority⏳ 6-month periods📏 General maximum: 3 years

356(1)(b)Declares that the State Legislature’s powers are exercisable by or under Parliament’s authority.Legislative takeover mechanism; operated through Art. 357.

🚨 Territorial and wording traps: a National Emergency in some unrelated part of India does not satisfy 356(5)(a); and the EC certifies difficulties in holding elections — do not rewrite it as “elections are impossible”.

⚙️ What changes in the State?

  • Executive: the ministry ordinarily ceases; administration runs on the President’s behalf, usually through the Governor.
  • Legislature: suspended animation or lawful dissolution; powers exercised by or under Parliament.
  • High Court: untouched. Rights: President’s Rule does not itself trigger Arts. 358–359.

⚖️ Federal firewall: S. R. Bommai (1994, nine judges)

🔍 Reviewable power

The proclamation is subject to judicial review. Courts examine whether relevant material existed, mala fides and impermissible use; the Union must justify the material. A bare claim of “satisfaction” is not conclusive.

🗳️ Floor test

A ministry’s majority should be tested on the floor of the House, not by the Governor’s private assessment.

🛡️ No premature dissolution

The Assembly should be kept in suspended animation until Parliament approves; if Parliament refuses, the Proclamation lapses and the Assembly revives.

🌐 Federalism & secularism

Both are basic features. Courts may invalidate an unconstitutional proclamation and, where suitable, restore the dismissed government and Assembly.

🧭 Breakdown ≠ bad politics. Political disagreement, an unfavourable election result or ordinary maladministration do not by themselves show that constitutional government is impossible. The Sarkaria Commission recommended using Art. 356 only as a last resort, after alternatives and (ordinarily) a warning, on a reasoned Governor’s report.
🧠 2–6–1–3 ladder2 months approval · 6-month periods · after 1 year: N + E (National Emergency + Election Commission) · 3-year ceiling.
Bommai anchors: R-F-M-R-S = Review · Floor test · Material · Restoration · Secularism.
ARTICLE357🖋️ Legislative Powers Under a 356 ProclamationDelegation · Union powers · expenditure · surviving laws

Where a 356 Proclamation declares that the State Legislature’s powers are exercisable by or under Parliament, Art. 357 supplies the machinery.

ClauseActual subjectInsight
357(1)(a)Parliament may confer the State Legislature’s law-making power on the President and authorise delegation to another specified authority.The delegation chain must rest on Parliament’s authorisation.
357(1)(b)Parliament, or the President/authority holding the power, may make laws conferring powers and imposing duties on the Union or its officers and authorities.This is not the expenditure clause.
357(1)(c)When the Lok Sabha is not in session, the President may authorise expenditure from the State Consolidated Fund pending Parliament’s sanction.Remember the “not in session” condition.
357(2)Laws made under this power that the maker would not otherwise be competent to make continue after the Proclamation ends until altered, repealed or amended by a competent Legislature or authority.No automatic six-month expiry.
🚨 Survival contrast: Art. 250 laws lose their otherwise-unsupported effect six months after the National Emergency ends; Art. 357(2) laws continue until competent alteration. Different rules, different Emergencies.

Connect with State budget provisions (Arts. 202–204) and the Consolidated Funds (Art. 266).

ARTICLE358🕊️ Article 19 During a Qualifying EmergencyWar or external aggression only · recital · automatic operation

While a Proclamation declaring a threat by war or external aggression is in operation, nothing in Art. 19 restricts the State’s power to make laws or take executive action that it would otherwise be competent to make or take. It does not operate for an Emergency based on armed rebellion alone.

ProvisionRuleMeaning
358(1)Art. 19 does not restrict State power during the qualifying Emergency; a law so made ceases to have effect, to the extent of incompetence, when the Proclamation ceases — except for things done or omitted earlier.Automatic — no separate Presidential order.
358(1) provisoFor a Proclamation confined to part of India, measures in another State need the specified security link.Conditional territorial spillover.
358(2)(a)No protection for a law lacking a recital that it relates to the Emergency in operation.Recital is essential.
358(2)(b)No protection for executive action not taken under a law containing the recital.No blanket executive immunity.

“Automatic” never means “unlimited”: ground, recital, territory and executive-authority conditions matter, and Art. 358 does not touch other rights.

🗣️ The six freedoms of Art. 19(1)

  1. (a) Speech and expression
  2. (b) Peaceful assembly without arms
  3. (c) Associations, unions, cooperative societies
  4. (d) Free movement throughout India
  5. (e) Residence and settlement
  6. (g) Profession, occupation, trade or business
🚨 Why does the lettering skip (f)? Art. 19(1)(f), the former right to property, was omitted by the 44th Amendment; property now sits in Art. 300A (Part XII).
🧠 S–A–A–M–R–P & 358 keySpeech · Assembly · Association · Movement · Residence · Profession.
358 = Art. 19 + (War or External aggression) + recital.
ARTICLE359⚖️ Enforcement of Part III RightsPresidential order · pending proceedings · Arts. 20 & 21 protected

While a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, the President may by order declare that the right to move any court for enforcement of such Part III rights as are mentioned in the order — except Articles 20 and 21 — and all pending proceedings for enforcing them, remain suspended for the Proclamation’s duration or a shorter specified period.

ClauseFunctionPrecision
359(1)Order suspending the right to move courts for the named rights (including pending proceedings).The exclusion of Arts. 20–21 is in the operative wording.
359(1A)While the order operates, the named rights do not restrict the State’s law-making and executive power; laws cease to have effect to the extent of incompetence when the order ceases (things done are saved).A limited substantive effect exists too (added by the 38th Amendment).
359(1A) provisoWhere the Proclamation is territorially limited, measures may apply elsewhere if the security link exists.Parallels Arts. 353 and 358.
359(1B)No protection for a law lacking the Emergency recital, or executive action not under such a law.The recital safeguard.
359(2)Order may extend to the whole or any part of India.Extension to a non-Emergency part needs the security link.
359(3)Every order must be laid before each House as soon as may be.Not an approval requirement.
🕰️ The order may last for the Emergency’s duration or a shorter period. It can follow any Art. 352 Emergency, including one based on armed rebellion. In Makhan Singh (1964) the Court held that only the named rights’ enforcement is suspended — challenges on other legal grounds are not automatically barred.
🚨 “Art. 359 suspends remedies, not rights” is a useful start but incomplete without 359(1A)–(1B).

🛡️ Art. 20 · criminal-law protections

No retrospective conviction or enhanced penalty; no double jeopardy; no compelled self-incrimination of an accused. Use the actual clauses — not a blanket slogan.

🕊️ Art. 21 · life & personal liberty

Deprivation only by procedure established by law (read with the fair, just and reasonable standard). Protects persons, not just citizens.

🧠 Order checklist · R–P–T–20/21Named Rights · stated Period · defined Territory · 20 & 21 excluded.
“358 remembers 19; 359 must spare 20 and 21.”
ARTICLE360💠 Financial EmergencyStability or credit · propriety directions · no periodic renewal

If the President is satisfied that a situation has arisen whereby the financial stability or credit of India or any part of its territory is threatened, a Proclamation may be made.

🏛️ Approval: 2 months🗳️ Ordinary majority🔁 No 6-month renewal⌛ No express maximum
ClauseRuleInsight
360(1)Ground and satisfaction.India or any part of its territory.
360(2)(a)May be revoked or varied by a later Proclamation.No approval needed for revocation.
360(2)(b)Laid before each House.Parliamentary scrutiny.
360(2)(c)Ceases after two months unless both Houses approve (Lok Sabha-dissolution proviso).Ordinary majority.
360(3)The Union’s executive authority extends to directing States to observe specified canons of financial propriety and other necessary directions.Supervision, not dismissal of State governments.
360(4)(a)(i)Directions may require reduction of salaries and allowances of persons serving in connection with State affairs.Directions-based, not automatic.
360(4)(a)(ii)May require Money Bills and other Bills to which Art. 207 applies to be reserved for the President after State passage.Do not widen to every State Bill.

  • It does not itself suspend Fundamental Rights (Arts. 358–359 link to Art. 352).
  • It does not require a prior National Emergency and does not automatically impose President’s Rule.
  • It is not synonymous with recession, inflation or a tight budget.
📌 Historical fact: no Financial Emergency has been proclaimed in India — not even in the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis. For any “as of today” question, confirm against the latest official record.
🧠 Financial Emergency in four wordsThreat → Propriety → Salaries → Bills. Duration: 2 months to approve; no periodic renewal.
🕊️ Act III · Liberty Under Pressure

Rights Do Not Vanish — Read the Exact Mechanism

The commonest error is treating Arts. 358 and 359 as identical or assuming every Emergency disables every right. They differ in trigger, instrument, rights affected and duration.

🚫 The inaccurate sentence

“During Emergency the government can suspend all Fundamental Rights and courts cannot interfere.”

✅ The accurate replacement

“During a National Emergency, Art. 358 may operate for war/external aggression; Art. 359 requires an order affecting enforcement of named rights and cannot include Arts. 20–21.”

🎛️ Act IV · The Comparison Deck

Three Alerts — One Control Panel

Memorise differences, not three disconnected descriptions. Choose an alert to see its dashboard.

Trigger: war, external aggression, armed rebellion.
  • Written Cabinet decision (352(3)); one-month approval by special majority; six-month renewals; no fixed maximum.
  • Lok Sabha disapproval route: one-tenth notice → special sitting within 14 days → mandatory revocation if passed.
  • States not dismissed; Union powers expand (353, 250); Arts. 358/359 may apply; terms extendable (83(2), 172(1)).
Trigger: State government cannot be carried on per the Constitution.
  • Governor’s report or otherwise; two-month approval by ordinary majority; six months from issue; three-year ceiling; extra tests beyond one year.
  • Ministry displaced; Legislature in suspended animation/dissolved; powers under Parliament (357); High Court untouched.
  • Reviewable (Bommai); floor test; no premature dissolution.
Trigger: financial stability or credit threatened.
  • Two-month approval by ordinary majority; no six-month renewal; no express maximum.
  • Directions on financial propriety; salary reductions (including judges via 360(4)(b)); reservation of certain State Bills.
  • Never proclaimed; no automatic rights suspension.

🕰️ Act VI · The Historical Record

Powers Evolved — So Did the Safeguards

India has seen three National Emergency proclamations (two overlapped in the 1970s), but well over a hundred instances of President’s Rule. President’s Rule was first imposed in Punjab in 1951.

63rd · 1989Reversed the 59th’s arrangements; omitted Art. 359A.359A is not operative today.
64th, 67th, 68thStepwise extension of President’s Rule in Punjab beyond three years (to a five-year outer limit).Do not generalise to other States.

🏛️ 44th: control of the proclamation

  • Ground narrowed: internal disturbance → armed rebellion.
  • Collective responsibility: written Cabinet decision.
  • Faster scrutiny: one month (was two).
  • Stronger vote: special majority.
  • Continuing control: six-month renewals + Lok Sabha disapproval.

🕊️ 44th: rights and federal safeguards

  • Arts. 20–21 excluded from Art. 359 orders.
  • Art. 358 limited to war/external aggression.
  • Recital and action-under-law conditions.
  • Art. 356: six-month periods restored; extension beyond one year constrained.
🔒 Art. 22 trap: Section 3 of the 44th Amendment (shortening the Advisory Board review period for preventive detention) has never been brought into force. Do not claim the operative Art. 22 uses its two-month rule. See A. K. Roy (1982).

🎞️ How judicial attitude evolved

1964 · MAKHAN SINGH

Art. 359 suspends only the enforcement of named rights. Other legal grounds are not automatically barred.

1976 · ADM JABALPUR

4–1 majority takes a highly restrictive view of habeas corpus. Khanna, J. dissents.

1977 · RAJASTHAN

Early, more deferential approach to Art. 356 satisfaction.

1980 · MINERVA MILLS

Limits on the amending power. Context for post-Emergency constitutional repair, not an Art. 352 case.

1994 · BOMMAI

Art. 356 becomes reviewable: material, floor test, no premature dissolution, federalism and secularism.

2006 · RAMESHWAR PRASAD

Bihar dissolution held unconstitutional; the Assembly was not restored because fresh elections had since been held.

2016–2017 · NABAM REBIA · PUTTASWAMY

Governors cannot act as political arbiters, and ADM Jabalpur is formally overruled.

⏳ Five sunset rules that must never be mixed

🖋️ Art. 250 laws

The otherwise-unsupported effect ceases six months after the National Emergency ends. Things already done are saved.

🏛️ Art. 357(2) laws

They continue until competently altered, repealed or amended. They do not expire automatically.

🕊️ Arts. 358 & 359(1A)

The extra State power ends with the qualifying proclamation (358) or the order (359). Things done earlier are saved.

💰 Art. 354 orders

The stated period cannot go beyond the end of the financial year in which the Emergency ceases.

🗳️ Arts. 83(2) & 172(1)

Term extension is by law, not more than one year at a time, and never beyond six months after the Emergency ceases.

🧭 How to read the result

Art. 358 is automatic but covers only Art. 19 and only for war or external aggression. Art. 359 needs a Presidential order naming rights and can never name Arts. 20 or 21.

Whatever the answer, verify four things: recital · territory · period · named rights.

🧯 Last resort, not first response

Ordinary policing, public-order law, lawful Union directions and legislative confidence tests are distinct from a proclamation under Art. 352 or 356. A serious problem does not automatically meet an Emergency threshold.

🏛️ Reform thinking: Article 356 as a last resort

📚 Sarkaria Commission (1983–88)

Recommended using Art. 356 very sparingly and only as a last resort. Alternatives should be tried first, and ordinarily a warning should be given to the State. The Governor’s report should be a reasoned, speaking document. The Assembly should not be dissolved before Parliament approves.

📚 Punchhi Commission (2007–10) & NCRWC (2002)

Both stressed codifying the Bommai safeguards and a reasoned Governor’s report. The Punchhi Commission also floated a time-limited localised emergency for a district or part of a State as an alternative to dismissing a whole government. This idea has never been enacted.

📌 Commission recommendations are advisory. The binding law is the constitutional text plus Bommai. Verify the exact wording of the reports before quoting them in an examination.
The constitutional question is not merely “Is there a crisis?” It is “Which lawful power addresses this crisis, under which conditions, for how long, and subject to whose scrutiny?”
